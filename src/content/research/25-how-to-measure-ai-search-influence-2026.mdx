**AI search influence** is not one number. It is a stack of three measurements — impressions inside generative answers, referral clicks from AI platforms, and citation presence in prompt outputs — each capturing a different stage of the answer-to-purchase journey, and each blind to non-click influence. ## The three layers

AI influence happens in three places, and none of the mainstream tools covers more than one of them.

1. **Impressions.** How often your URLs appear inside a generative answer. First-party view: Google's new **Generative AI performance report** (Search Console, launched June 3, 2026) covers AI Overviews and AI Mode, by page, country, device, and date.
2. **Referral clicks.** When a user actually follows a cited link. GA4 captures these — partially — via a native **AI Assistant** channel added May 13, 2026, plus a custom regex channel for the engines it misses (see below).
3. **Citation presence.** Whether your brand is named and linked in answers that never produce a click. No first-party tool reports this; it is measured with fixed prompt sets run across engines, manually or with trackers like Ahrefs Brand Radar, Profound, or Otterly.

The reason all three are required is that they measure different phenomena. GA4 tells you who clicked through; the GSC report tells you where Google chose to show you. **Neither tells you whether ChatGPT is recommending you for queries where nobody clicks** — which is where most AI influence on discovery actually happens (Pew measured users clicking a cited source just 1% of the time in AI summaries). Citation presence is the leading indicator; referral traffic is the trailing one.

## The blind spot: the report built for AI visibility cannot see AI queries

Here is the 2026 inversion that most teams trip on. Google released the Generative AI performance report specifically to isolate AI visibility — and gave it **no Queries dimension at all**. Its dimensions are Pages, Countries, Devices, and Dates. No queries, no clicks, no CTR, no position. It tells you *which* pages Google surfaced in AI Overviews and AI Mode; it cannot tell you *why* — the queries that produced them.

Meanwhile, the queries themselves show up somewhere else entirely. Because Google counts **every follow-up question in AI Mode as a brand-new query**, conversational fragments — "yes", "yes go on", "yes, pricing" — land as rows in the **main** Performance report, mixed in with your classic searches and carrying no AI label. Practitioners watched these appear through mid-2026 (a documented, intended behavior). You can pattern-match them with a regex on conversational strings, but you cannot clean them into a segment.

Two further limits, both first-party:

- **The anonymized pool.** Google withholds rare queries from reporting; one practitioner measured **57.7% of impressions sitting in the anonymized pool** over 59 days (Aug 2026). The fragments you can see are an undercount.
- **The counting rules.** In AI Overviews, a link must be scrolled or expanded into view before it counts as an impression; in AI Mode, each turn re-counts. Compare exports across consistent periods rather than trusting daily totals.

Practically: use the Generative AI report as a **diagnostic of where Google is willing to surface you**, and the main Performance report — with its living AI-conversation rows — as a journey signal. A "yes, pricing" turn is a late-funnel conversational breadcrumb; the teams that read it as such are building answer-first chunks for the follow-up turns, not just the opening question.

## GA4: native channel, mended with regex

GA4's native AI Assistant channel covers **ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude only**, has no historical backfill before May 13, 2026, and still loses sessions whose referrer is stripped by mobile apps or privacy browsers. **Perplexity and Copilot remain buried in Referral** unless you add a custom channel group.

The fix is ten minutes: copy the default channel group, add a channel named "AI referrals" above Referral (channel rules evaluate in order — placed below, Referral wins), and set its source regex to something like `chatgpt\.com|chat\.openai\.com|perplexity\.ai|gemini\.google\.com|claude\.ai|copilot\.microsoft\.com`. Then keep your expectations honest:

- ChatGPT only began appending `utm_source` in June 2025; Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode pass **no attribution data at all** and report as ordinary organic.
- One estimate places unattributed AI traffic as high as **70.6%**.
- When you do see AI referral traffic, expect a conversion-rate story, not a volume story: Seer measured **15.9% AI-referral conversion versus 1.76% organic** on one B2B site — a fraction of the volume at several times the rate.

There is one free first-party reporting exception worth exploiting: **Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report** (public preview February 2026; June 2026 added Intents, Topics, Citation Share, and Compare views) shows which URLs Copilot and Bing's AI answers cite. Because ChatGPT's live search runs on the Bing index, Copilot citation is a leading indicator for ChatGPT too.

## Mention ≠ citation ≠ click

The cleanest recent dataset on this is Semrush's 50,000-brand study: in **79% of categories, the most-cited domain is not the most-mentioned brand**. An engine can cite a review site's page *about* you (citation of their domain, mention of your brand) or name you from memory with no source at all (mention, no citation). Merge the two in a spreadsheet and you will misread your position in both directions. And across 53.7% of categories, there was **no dominant brand at all** in ChatGPT answers — "nobody consistently" is the normal state for most of the web.

Track four ratios against a fixed prompt set, per engine:

- **Mention rate** — share of runs naming your brand. The baseline for "does the model know you exist."
- **Citation rate** — share of runs citing a URL from your domain.
- **Share of voice** — your citations ÷ total citations in the same prompt set. The competitive number; per-engine splits are mandatory because ChatGPT and Perplexity draw from different corpora (our [divergence study](/resources/research/ai-mode-ai-overviews-13-percent-overlap-2026) measured ~11% domain overlap).
- **Sentiment / framing** — recommended, neutral, or caveated.

The single most useful output is the **gap matrix**: every prompt where you are absent, paired with the sources the engine cites instead. Indexly's data adds a structural lever: brands cited in at least three independent sources are extracted by AI at **3× the rate** of brands with no off-site mentions. And ranking gives you retrieval, not citation — Semrush found 85% of pages ChatGPT retrieves are never cited.

## The operational stack

- **Cadence:** monthly prompt capture, quarterly trend review. Weekly numbers are noise — retrieval drifts, personalization residue, sampling variance. Monthly produces signal; react on the quarter.
- **Prompt library:** 20–50 prompts across branded, category, comparison, and problem-intent, written in real buyer language (support tickets and search console data, not marketing copy). Refresh quarterly.
- **Alerts:** a citation-rate drop above ~20% week-over-week, share-of-voice drop above ~5%, or a competitor breaking your top-3 co-mentions triggers investigation — usually an engine update or a redistribution event, not a content regression.
- **Tooling thresholds:** manual works for one brand and a few engines. Automate when manual exceeds ≈3 hours/month or you track 5+ competitors (entry tiers ~$29–99/month; serious platforms $249–499+).
- **Dashboard:** present — inclusion rate, citation coverage, share of voice by engine, answer placement. Do not present a single blended score.

The honest summary, from the [year in review](/resources/research/what-changed-ai-search-2026-year-in-review): measurement closed partway in 2026 — first-party AI reporting exists, but it is impressions-only, unsegmented, and anonymized at the margin. The teams that track citation rate, share of voice, and attribution by engine hold the private dataset the public reports do not.
