<DefinitionCard>
**Entity corroboration** is the state where independent sources — your site's schema, Wikidata, Wikipedia, review sites, and editorial media — describe the same entity with the same facts. AI systems check it *before* they evaluate content quality: a page from a recognizable, corroborated entity gets past the retrieval filter; a page from a blurred one does not, no matter how well written.
</DefinitionCard>

This article extends the [entity optimization guide](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026) and the [AI Citation Levers hub](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026). It pairs with the [Reddit citation study](/resources/research/reddit-community-ai-citations-2026) because the two platforms anchor opposite engines.

## The spine

Across the largest 2026 syntheses of AI citation data, two domains dominate in a way that resembles infrastructure more than content marketing.

5W's Q1 2026 Citation Source Audit — a synthesis of nine independent datasets covering **680M+ tracked citations** — found **Wikipedia (13.15%) and Reddit (11.97%) together account for more than 25% of ChatGPT citations in the US** (Similarweb panel, Jan–Feb 2026, ~600,000 citation events). Within ChatGPT's ten most-referenced domains, **Wikipedia alone holds 47.9% of citations**; Reddit is a distant second near 12%.

Resocial's weighted cross-engine analysis reaches the same conclusion from the other direction: **Wikipedia ~32% of citations by weighted average**, Reddit 21%, tier-1 editorial 14%, brand-owned content 11%, government/academic 8%.

The radical part of the 5W finding: **the Wall Street Journal, the New York Times, and Bloomberg do not appear in the top 20.** The traditional PR tier system — the "get picked up in a business paper" playbook — does not map to how AI engines source answers. The structural break is real, and it reframes who the "greatest authorities" are in the answer layer.

## Entity corroboration is the pre-filter

The mechanism that makes Wikipedia matter is upstream of citations entirely.

Radiant Elephant's read of retrieval-augmented generation pipelines is the clearest statement of it: **before an AI system evaluates whether a page's content is good enough to cite, it evaluates whether it understands what entity the page represents.** Is this from a real company? A verifiable author? Does the brand exist as a recognized entity in the knowledge graph? A page that fails this first filter is never evaluated for content at all.

This is why Wikipedia appears in virtually every LLM training dataset — roughly 3% of GPT-3's training data traces to it — and why academic retrieval systems (REALM, DPR) use Wikipedia explicitly because it demonstrably reduces hallucinations. When an AI can cross-reference a claim against a stable reference entry, it gains confidence. When it cannot, it hedges or omits.

In Kalicube's framing, corroboration is the "Grounded" gate: one source asserting something is a claim; two confirming it is corroboration; **three independent, high-confidence sources converging is consensus — and at consensus the AI stops saying "claims to be" and starts saying "is."** Below the threshold it hedges; above it, it asserts.

## The four-node chain

Entity corroboration is a chain, not a single asset. Each node works on a different part of the retrieval stack:

| Node | What it does | Engine layer it feeds |
|------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Organization schema + sameAs** | Declares "this page = this real-world entity" in code | Recognition and disambiguation |
| **Wikidata (QID)** | Machine-readable identity, no notability bar | Knowledge graph grounding |
| **Wikipedia article** | Independent verification, structured reference | Pre-training weight + RAG retrieval |
| **Third-party set** | Editorial, reviews, directories, registries | Grounding confidence ("is" vs "claims to be") |

The chain propagates. RanketAI describes the pathway explicitly: **Wikidata → Google Knowledge Graph → AI Overview → Gemini.** Improve one Wikidata property and it can flow through the entire downstream stack. Their summary for organizations is blunt: *one accurate Wikidata entry can outperform hundreds of content pieces* — because the entity graph determines whether your content is even eligible to be retrieved.

## Wikidata is the low-barrier entry

The practical question for most brands — particularly mid-market, healthcare, legal, and finance — is that Wikipedia's notability bar is genuinely high: in-depth independent coverage in reliable sources is required, and self-editing gets reverted.

**Wikidata is different.** It applies no notability threshold. Any registered editor can create a well-referenced item for a legitimate organization: instance-of, official website, founding date, headquarters — each property backed by a reference. Because properties without references can be deleted, the references are the actual work.

The measured effects:

- **3.2× more likely to earn a knowledge panel** with a Wikidata entry (Kalicube, 75,000+ tracked brands).
- **2.7× more likely to appear in AI Overview citations** with Wikidata verification — independent of organic ranking position.
- Ahrefs' case study: its Wikidata entry was indexed the **same day** its Organization schema added the Wikidata sameAs link; a knowledge panel appeared within **7 days**; and the panel **remained after the Wikipedia and Wikidata pages were removed** — the graph anchor is sticky.
- An LSE experiment on thesis records found **47% more downloads and doubled Wikipedia traffic** from Wikidata exposure alone.

The sameAs array is the connector: it is the "entity canonical." One Organization schema block listing the QID, the LinkedIn profile, and the Crunchbase page tells the retrieval stack that the entity on your site is the same entity defined in trusted public databases. Schema App's controlled case study isolated sameAs as the variable and measured immediate entity-confidence gains.

## Wikipedia is ChatGPT's spine, not everyone's

The engine differentiation matters for budgeting. Conductor's seven-month citation study (1,056 data points, Sep 2025–Mar 2026) found **ChatGPT and ChatGPT Search are the only Wikipedia-anchored engines in the dataset** — ChatGPT Search cited Wikipedia first for the Education intent in every one of the seven months.

The per-engine Wikipedia shares tell the same story:

| Engine | Wikipedia share (within top-10 cited / all) |
|--------|---------------------------------------------|
| ChatGPT | 47.9% top-10; ~7.8–12.1% all |
| Gemini | ~38% |
| Claude | ~41% (Wikipedia + tier-1 editorial weighted) |
| Google AI Overviews | ~25% |
| Perplexity | ~12.4% top-10 (Reddit-led at 46.7%) |

Perplexity and ChatGPT are near-inverse on the Wikipedia-vs-Reddit axis. A brand visible in one is often invisible in the other — the reason cited cross-engine overlap is only ~11% of domains. If your buyers research on ChatGPT, Wikipedia is the single highest-leverage asset available. If they use Perplexity, the Reddit playbook matters more than the entity spine.

## The concentration risk

The dominance of two domains is also a fragility. Semrush's three-month, 230,000-prompt study found ChatGPT **actively redistributing away from single-source over-reliance**: Reddit fell from 60% to 10% of responses within six weeks, and Wikipedia's share of responses fell from 55% to under 20% during the same period — a shift isolated to ChatGPT, while Perplexity and AI Mode stayed stable.

Two lessons follow. First, **retrieval is not citation**: Semrush also found 85% of pages ChatGPT retrieves are never actually cited. Being retrieved by the engine is the floor; corroboration decides whether you clear the citation bar. Second, **depth of corroboration is the hedge**: the two-domain concentration is real but actively managed by the platforms. Brand strategy built on a single Wikipedia article is exposed; a corroboration set across Wikidata, editorial, reviews, and community survives the redistribution.

## Practical playbook

**1. Audit your entity first.** Search your brand across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and AI Mode. Note where answers hedge ("claims to be"), contradict, or fail to recognize you. That is the corroboration gap.

**2. Build the Wikidata node (Day 1).** Create or verify the QID with referenced properties: instance of, founded, headquarters, official website. Bidirectionally link any Wikipedia article.

**3. Wire the sameAs array (Day 1–2).** Organization schema with Wikidata, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, and social profiles. Link founder/executive Person schema to their entities.

**4. Decide on Wikipedia with honesty.** If notability fails, spend the eligibility months earning qualifying coverage instead of gambling on deletion-prone drafts. Content you cannot source dies on the platform.

**5. Corroborate with 30+ sources.** The knowledge-panel confidence threshold in Kalicube's analysis sits around 30 independent authoritative sources — a spread of editorial, directories, reviews, and registries describing you identically.

**6. Match the engine to your buyers.** ChatGPT-heavy audiences justify the full Wikipedia + Wikidata build; Perplexity-heavy audiences shift budget to community and in-language sources.
