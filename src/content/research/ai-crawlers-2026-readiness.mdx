In our [article about who gets cited](/resources/research/2026-ai-citation-study), one
finding kept insisting on being mentioned: the technical one. When OtterlyAI analyzed a
million-citation sample, roughly **73% of sites carried at least one technical barrier**
that could prevent them from being cited — most commonly content that only exists after
client-side JavaScript runs. In other words, most of the conversation about "AI SEO" is
happening upstream of the real gate, which is: **can the agent read you at all?**

This article is the companion piece: who the major AI crawlers are in 2026, why robots.txt
is a weaker lever than the industry assumes, and a concrete readiness playbook you can run
today against your own site.

## The crawlers worth knowing

Every major assistant operates a retrieval crawler that can pull your pages into its
answers, plus separate tuning crawlers that feed model training. The names that matter:

| User-agent | Operator | What it feeds | Honours robots.txt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `GPTBot`, `OAI-SearchBot` | OpenAI | ChatGPT answers and ChatGPT Search | Named bots respect it |
| `ClaudeBot` | Anthropic | Claude answers | Respects it |
| `PerplexityBot` | Perplexity | Perplexity answers | Respects it |
| `Google-Extended` | Google | Gemini grounding and AI training | Respects it |
| `Googlebot` | Google | Search, AI Overviews, AI Mode | Respects it |
| `Applebot-Extended` | Apple | Siri and Apple Intelligence | Respects it |
| `Bingbot` | Microsoft | Copilot answers | Respects it |
| `Bytespider` | ByteDance | Doubao / TikTok search | Historically imperfect |
| `Amazonbot` | Amazon | Rufus and Alexa | Respects it |
| `Meta-ExternalAgent` | Meta | Meta AI | Respects it |
| `CCBot` | Common Crawl | Third-party model training corpora | Respects it |

Two practical points stand out. First, there are two waves of the same company: the
**grounding crawler** that retrieves for live answers (most likely to cite you) and the
**training crawler** that copies for model weight (most likely to be worth blocking if you
have licensing concerns). `robots.txt` lets you separate them — `GPTBot` from
`OAI-Datalyzer`, `Google-Extended` from `Googlebot`. Second, some traffic still arrives from
unlisted or disclosed fetchers that will not honor robots.txt, so never build your strategy
on the assumption that robots.txt is a complete shield or a complete door.

For reference, our own [robots.txt](https://promptco.online/robots.txt) is deliberately
minimal and AI-friendly: universal crawl allowed, with only `/api/`, `/admin/` and
`/_next/` excluded, and explicit `Allow` entries for OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot,
PerplexityBot, Applebot-Extended and `anthropic-ai`.

## Why robots.txt is not the lever people think it is

Three well-documented truths, in order of how often they are ignored:

- **robots.txt cannot make you more visible — it can only stop blocking you.** Allow-listing
  an assistant's crawler does not increase your odds of citation; actively blocking it can
  zero them out. The default assumption should be *allow and measure*, not *deny and brace*.
- **Search-facing AI surfaces do not always follow robots.txt.** Google has been explicit
  that robots.txt is not designed to govern every AI experience, and AI-assisted results
  can draw on independently indexed content. Treat your robots file as a boundary for the
  agents that honor it, and as nothing more.
- **The community-contributed `llms.txt` file carries no special ranking weight.** Google
  has said so directly. It remains a useful, human-maintained map of your content for
  curious agents — but it is not a visibility lever.

So the answer to "should I spend the week perfecting robots.txt?" is: spend 20 minutes
deleting anything that blocks the grounding crawlers above, then spend the rest of the week
on the levers below — because that is where citations are actually lost and won.

## The technical levers that decide whether agents can read you

The research on retrieval behavior points to a short checklist that separates the sites
agents can reliably parse from the ones carrying barriers:

**1. Content must exist in the initial server-rendered HTML.** This is the big one. If your
product UI is fine as a client-side React/Vue/SPA shell but your content only hydrates after
JavaScript, some agents — especially ones doing cheap or policy-restricted fetches — never
see it. Static pre-rendering, server rendering, or SSG (like this site's [research
pages](/resources/research)) puts the body in the raw HTML.

**2. One URL must equal one distinct topic.** Retrieval systems budget limited fetches per
query. Public measurements found an assistant retrieving on the order of a hundred pages
per query while only a fraction of them surfaced into the final answer (Tw93, 2026). Pages
that blend many topics into one URL make it easy for an agent to spend a fetch and find
nothing directly quotable. Clean, single-topic URLs win.

**3. Responses must be fast.** Agent research in 2026 repeatedly flags latency as a filter:
assistants favour and cache pages that serve fast, and sub-600ms time-to-first-byte on the
edge is the commonly cited target. If you are on a shared host with slow TLS or no edge
cache, that is a readiness problem, not a luxury problem.

**4. Depth matters more than breadth of navigation.** Agent-friendly crawling is shallow:
landing-page-to-content in two or three clicks beats a giant taxonomy that buries the
useful page six levels deep. If the route to your best content requires navigating a
dropdown tree, flatten it.

**5. Block the wrong things.** Verify that your security layer (CDN, WAF, bot-challenge) is
not challenging the named AI user-agents. A "yes we allow GPTBot" robots.txt means nothing
if a managed challenge or bot-detection script is sending `GPTBot` to a CAPTCHA page.

**6. Make structured data factual and complete.** AI features and answer engines use schema
for classification far more than for weighting — wrong or inflated schema can actively
misclassify your content. Order of effort: `Organization`, `BreadcrumbList`, `WebPage`,
`HowTo`/`FAQPage` where true, then per-page `Article`/`BlogPosting`. Never markup what the
page does not actually contain.

**7. Freshness is measured, not promised.** As we covered in [the citation
study](/resources/research/2026-ai-citation-study), content updated in roughly the last
month is cited at materially higher rates in large samples. Real `lastModified` signals and
visible changes are what modern evidence rewards.

**8. Sitemaps are the cheap win.** A correct `sitemap.xml` with canonical URLs and dates is
the single cheapest way to tell every agent what your most important pages are. Ours is
generated from the same registry that drives our [research index](/resources/research), so
the two can never drift apart.

## A 10-point self-audit you can run today

1. `curl -s https://yoursite.com | grep -c "<article\|<h1\|<p"` — does the raw HTML contain
   real content beyond the app shell?
2. View the page with JavaScript disabled. Is the answer to your core query still visible?
3. `curl -sA "GPTBot" https://yoursite.com/page` — does the agent user-agent get the same
   200 as a browser, or a challenge page?
4. Run the same user-agent test for `ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended`,
   `Applebot-Extended` and `CCBot`.
5. Confirm your robots.txt does not `Disallow:` the grounding crawlers listed above by
   accident (shared `User-agent: *` rules included).
6. Confirm `/sitemap.xml` returns 200, is referenced by robots.txt, and lists every page you
   want referenced — dated and canonical.
7. Check that the pages which matter return in under ~600ms time-to-first-byte from a
   distant edge node.
8. Validate your structured data against a validator; fix warnings rather than silencing
   them.
9. Confirm the most important content is reachable from the homepage in two to three clicks.
10. Re-run this audit monthly. Citation pools churn on ~4-week windows — your readiness
    check should too. The [citation study](/resources/research/2026-ai-citation-study) is
    the reading list for what happens once agents can actually parse you.
