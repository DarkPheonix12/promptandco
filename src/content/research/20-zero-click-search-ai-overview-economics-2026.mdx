<DefinitionCard>
**Zero-click search** is a search that ends inside the results page — the user reads the answer (an AI Overview, featured snippet, or knowledge panel) and never clicks out to a source. In 2026, it is the majority outcome: 68.01% of US Google searches. **Zero-click marketing** is the strategic response: optimizing for citation inside the answer, not the click beneath it.
</DefinitionCard>

## The 68% headline

The anchoring number of the 2026 search economy comes from SparkToro, built on Similarweb's clickstream panel: **68.01% of US Google searches ended without a click** in January–April 2026 (Rand Fishkin, Jun 8 2026). For context:

| Year | US zero-click share | Basis |
|------|--------------------|-------|
| 2016 | ~45% | Jumpshot panel |
| 2019 | ~49% | Jumpshot panel |
| 2024 | 60.45% | Datos panel |
| 2026 (Jan–Apr) | **68.01%** | Similarweb panel |

The 2024-to-2026 move — +7.56 points in two years — is the steepest in the metric's history. The more operational framing: for every 1,000 US searches, only **232 clicks now reach the open web**, down from 374 two years earlier. The pool of clickable attention shrank ~26% in two years.

Two nuances matter before anyone calls "SEO is dead." First, the share of searches producing at least one click fell 9.51 points — a 22.9% contraction — which is roughly what you would expect if your rankings held flat while sessions fell. Second, the acceleration is primarily behavioral: Google's in-SERP answering features matured for a decade, and AI Overviews are the most aggressive of them.

## Three methods, one conclusion

The claim that AI Overviews suppress clicks is now triangulated by three independent methods. No single one is decisive; the convergence is.

**Pew panel (Mar 2025 data).** Users who saw an AI summary clicked a traditional result on **8% of visits**; users who did not, **15%** — a ~47% relative drop. Clicks on the sources cited inside the summary occurred on just **1% of visits**. The same panel found 26% of sessions ended right after an AI Overview page, versus 16% without.

**Ahrefs counterfactual (Dec 2025).** Comparing 150,000 keywords with an AI Overview against 150,000 informational keywords without: position-one CTR on AIO keywords fell from 0.073 to 0.016. Adjusted against a non-AIO counterfactual, **an AI Overview reduces position-one click-through by 58%** — up from 34.5% when the same team ran the study in April 2025. The suppression is intensifying as the feature matures.

**Randomized field experiment (Agarwal & Sen, SSRN Apr 2026).** 1,065 US participants were randomly assigned to see or not see AI Overviews for two weeks in January–February 2026. Organic clicks dropped **38%**, and zero-click searches jumped from **54% to 72%**.

The three studies measure different things — per-session click probability, position-one CTR, and causal response — and they agree: AI Overviews remove a large share of clicks. The mechanism, per Pew, is that the answer makes the click optional, and the citation links recover a rounding error, not a traffic stream.

## Two scoreboards

The zero-click shift is not the death of SEO. It is the split of search into **two scoreboards** that measure different wins:

| Scoreboard | Unit of success | Where you win | Declining or growing |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Clicks** | Sessions, CTR | Blue links, paid, Google properties | Declining (~22.9% in 2 years) |
| **Citations** | Mention share inside AI answers | AI Overview, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini | Growing (AI surfaces only ~0.34%→now scaling) |

Most teams still run one scoreboard: Search Console clicks or GA sessions. That scoreboard now under-counts the influence you actually have — because a zero-click answer that quotes your brand still shapes the buyer's shortlist, even though analytics records nothing.

The clearest evidence that citation and ranking are different contests: the overlap between ranking in Google's top 10 and being cited inside an AI answer has fallen from roughly 75% (mid-2025) to between **17% and 38%** (early 2026). Ranking #1 and being the answer's source have become two different jobs.

## Cited brands get more clicks

Here is the counterintuitive part: **citation is not the death of clicks — it is a click amplifier for the cited.** Seer Interactive's long-running panel (3,119 informational keywords, 25.1M organic impressions, Jun 2024–Feb 2026) found brands cited in AI Overviews earn **+120% more organic clicks per impression** than non-cited sources for the same queries (Apr 2026 update).

The mechanism: an AI Overview strangles clicks for everyone *except* the one or two brands inside the answer. Those brands capture a disproportionate share of what remains — and in AI Mode, where 93% of sessions never leave the interface, the cited source is the only visibility that exists.

So the operating rule of 2026 is: **rank for the click AND engineer for the citation.** The survivors of zero-click search are the cited.

## The three-zone portfolio

Similarweb's keyword-level zero-click data makes this operational. Zero-click rate is now reported per keyword, which lets you sort your entire portfolio by what each query is: a click asset or a presence asset.

| Zero-click rate | Query type | Play | Optimize for |
|-----------------|-----------|------|--------------|
| 0–30% | Commercial, navigational, high-intent | **Click** | CTR, title, meta, compelling result |
| 30–70% | Mixed / informational-commercial | **Hybrid** | Both citation readiness and click-worthiness |
| 70–100% | Pure informational, factual, definitional | **Citation** | AEO/GEO only; traffic is not the metric |

Two consequences follow. First, transactional query types ("buy X", "best X for Y") trigger full AI answers only ~3–4% of the time — the click layer survives where money changes hands. Second, informational "how does X work" queries resolve without a click at ~99.9% — optimizing those for CTR is optimizing for a click that no longer exists.

The portfolio implication: most B2B content budgets are still upside down — most resources go to informational pages (citation zone) measured as if they were click assets.

## The AI Mode measurement blind spot

People who theorized AI Mode caused the 68% were wrong for 2026 — it was 0.34% of searches. But the future is AI Mode: **over one billion monthly users** at I/O 2026, queries more than doubling each quarter, ~93% zero-click, and referral rates of 1.6–2.5% versus 17–19% for traditional search.

The measurement problem: you cannot see it in analytics. But you *can* see it in Search Console. Google confirmed in August 2026 that **AI Overviews and AI Mode data lands in the regular performance report**, with full queries, positions, and clicks — while the separate generative AI report (launched June 2026) shows only aggregated impressions. AI Mode follow-up questions count as new queries.

The tell: ultra-short fragments that nobody would type — "yes", "pricing", "go on". A query like **"yes, pricing" at position 1** is not a bug; it is a follow-up inside an AI Mode session, meaning your content was inside the AI answer the user was reading. Follow-up queries inflate impressions and depress average CTR — if you see them, your total visibility is growing even as the ratio looks sick.

## Google's own concession

Google's May 6 2026 updates to AI Overviews and AI Mode are the clearest acknowledgment of the click pressure: **inline links next to bullet points** (previously tucked in a source bar), **hover previews**, a **"Subscribed" label** for news subscriptions, and an "Explore new angles" section of article suggestions at the end of answers.

These are link-visibility tweaks at the UX layer. They improve the *attribution* of an answer, not its traffic. Google can make the consolation prize (Pew's 1% citation-click rate) slightly better paid, but it does not change the fundamental mechanic: a complete AI answer makes the click optional. Teams planning on a traffic recovery from these updates are planning for a scenario the data does not support.

## Recency is a lever, and citations decay

One operational finding changes how quickly to act. Per Cite Solutions' analysis, **the average AI citation loses half its visibility in about 4.5 weeks.** Engines pick the freshest credible source, not the oldest authoritative one. Combined with Seer's finding that cited brands out-earn non-cited brands, the recipe is: pages that are cited must also be *refreshed* to stay cited. Summertime numbers do not survive an e-commerce season.

## Practical playbook

**1. Run both scoreboards.** Report citation share and prompt coverage as a standalone channel with its own targets, next to organic traffic. What gets reported gets funded.

**2. Segment by zero-click rate.** Split your keyword portfolio into click, hybrid, and citation zones. Measure each with the scoreboard that matches its zone.

**3. Lead with the answer.** Each page gets one direct 40–60 word answer to its target question, placed high, in plain language. Engines extract passages, not narratives.

**4. Engineer for citation, then for the click it amplifies.** For the cited brand, clicks are up 120% per impression — so citation readiness is also click strategy.

**5. Watch the follow-up queries.** In Search Console, look for ultra-short fragments at position 1. Those are your AI Mode thermometer.

**6. Refresh the cited.** The 4.5-week half-life means the pages you want quoted need regular updates before their claims go stale.
