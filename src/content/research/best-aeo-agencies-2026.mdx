## How AEO differs from GEO — and why it matters for hiring

Before the map: the acronyms are not interchangeable, and an agency that treats them as synonyms is
telling you something.

- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)** is output-focused: getting your content *selected as the
  direct answer* in structured answer surfaces — featured snippets, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode,
  voice responses, and the extractive surfaces assistants use.
- **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)** is pipeline-focused: engineering content to survive
  retrieval, extraction and compression inside *generated* answers — being named, cited or
  recommended by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and their peers.

The work overlaps heavily — crawlability, entity clarity, extractable structure — but the measurement
differs (answer presence vs. citation presence), and the restructuring targets differ (answer formats
vs. quotable passages). Our [taxonomy decoder](/resources/research/seo-geo-aeo-llmo-guide-2026)
covers the full four-acronym picture.

**The hiring implication:** ask any agency you evaluate which surfaces your buying questions actually
trigger, and what they measure on each. If the answer is "AI search" applied uniformly to everything,
you are talking to a generalist with a vocabulary, not a specialist with a method.

## What to actually look for when evaluating

The questions that separate real AEO work from rebranded SEO:

1. **Baseline before pitch.** Do they measure current answer presence across defined prompts and
   surfaces before selling a program — and can they show the method?
2. **Named surfaces, not generic "AI."** Featured snippets, AI Overviews, AI Mode, voice,
   assistant answers — which ones does your market trigger, and what do they measure on each?
3. **AEO/GEO distinction.** Can they explain what they'd do differently for an answer surface vs.
   a generated answer? If the acronyms are interchangeable to them, the method is too.
4. **Extraction-format competence.** Do they restructure content into answer-ready form — direct
   answers near the top, lists, tables, FAQ blocks — or just publish more pages?
5. **Proof you can inspect.** Real client evidence with verifiable numbers, or placeholder
   percentages with no named clients?
6. **Pricing honesty.** Market ranges distinguished from their own rates; scoping before quoting.
7. **The guarantee test.** Guaranteed placements or "#1 in AI search" promises mean walk away.

## The red flags that mean "rebranded SEO"

- Fixed-price AEO tiers before any audit or baseline.
- "Guaranteed AI Overview placement" or "#1 in AI search" promises.
- Case studies with invented percentages and no named clients.
- No mention of specific surfaces, answer formats, or measurement cadence.
- An AEO page that is a thin rehash of the agency's SEO page with "AI" inserted.
- No published content demonstrating answer-surface understanding beyond marketing vocabulary.

One red flag alone may be a communication issue. Several together are a pattern.
