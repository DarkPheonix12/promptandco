The B2B buyer journey has compressed from weeks of research into a single AI chatbot prompt that returns a shortlist — and 69% of buyers chose a different vendor than they originally planned because of what that prompt returned.

The shortlist no longer forms on your website. It forms inside a ChatGPT window your sales team cannot see, powered by sources your marketing team does not control, before a single form fill, demo request, or site visit occurs. G2 calls this the Answer Economy. The data from 2026 makes clear why every B2B revenue team needs a new playbook.

## The Third Compression

Tim Sanders, G2's Chief Innovation Officer, frames the shift in one sentence: "The Yellow Pages compressed the market into the big book. Google compressed it into the first page of results. Now, AI chatbots are compressing it into a single answer" (G2, Apr 2026).

The numbers behind that compression are staggering. In March 2026, G2 surveyed 1,076 B2B software buyers and decision-makers across North America, EMEA, and APAC. Half (51%) now start their software research with an AI chatbot more often than Google — up from 29% just eleven months earlier (G2, Apr 2026). Nearly three in four (71%) rely on AI chatbots somewhere in their research process, up from 60% seven months prior. And 53% say their software research is more productive with AI than traditional search, nearly doubling from 36% (G2, Apr 2026).

Buyers have not stopped Googling — 80% still use Google somewhere in their journey. But they increasingly see it as a complementary function. The first move, for half of B2B buyers, is now a chatbot prompt.

## From Reference to Inference

The deeper shift is behavioral, not just channel-based. G2 frames it as a move "from reference to inference." Buyers used to ask search engines to point them toward sources, then synthesized the answer themselves. Now they tell a model to synthesize everything and return the shortlist in one prompt.

Two-thirds of buyers start with category or competitor queries — "best CRM for a 50-person SaaS company," "alternatives to [competitor]," "compare [vendor A] vs [vendor B]." These are evaluation prompts designed to return a ranked list of vendors. Only one in five start with questions about requirements or process (G2, Apr 2026). Buyers arrive at the chatbot with commercial intent from the first keystroke.

The process moves fast. Four out of five buyers say AI chatbots accelerated their purchasing decision, and 83% report feeling more confident in their final choice (G2, Apr 2026). Comparing vendor strengths and weaknesses is the number-one use case for AI chatbots in software research at 41%, ahead of basic product research, vendor identification, and use case validation (G2, Apr 2026).

## The Shortlist Forms Before You Know It Exists

This is the structural disruption: shortlist formation now happens off-site, before any tracked touchpoint. G2's data shows AI chatbots are the number-one source influencing buyer shortlists at 54%, ahead of software review sites at 43% and vendor websites at 36% (IvrisTech analysis of G2 data, Apr 2026).

The consequences are measurable. 69% of buyers chose a different vendor than they initially planned based on AI chatbot guidance. One in three (33%) purchased from a vendor they had never previously heard of before the AI surfaced them (G2, Apr 2026). Simply being mentioned carries weight: 85% of buyers think more highly of a vendor when an AI chatbot includes them in a recommendation (G2, Apr 2026).

The inverse is the part that should keep CMOs up at night. If AI leaves you out of the response, the buyer may never learn you exist. The brand equity you built over a decade can be flattened by a chatbot that did not think to mention you.

The average B2B shortlist has contracted from approximately 3.2 names to 2.5 in 2026 (Apollo data, cited by MarketScale, Jul 2026). Fewer candidates means each slot carries more weight, and AI tools are now the primary mechanism determining who earns those slots. 6sense data shows that 80% of the time, the vendor a buyer privately favors before first contact goes on to win the deal (6sense, 2025). The competitive battle is largely over before sales teams enter it.

## The Review-AI Flywheel

If AI builds the shortlist, what makes AI confident enough to name you? The answer is peer reviews — and the data shows a compounding flywheel.

When G2 asked buyers what would increase their confidence in an AI chatbot's answer, the number-one response was a citation from a review site (G2, Apr 2026). Review sites are the only source besides AI chatbots that gains influence as buyers move deeper into the funnel, growing from 40% at discovery to 47% at retention (G2, Apr 2026). When an AI chatbot conflicts with a brand a buyer trusts, 24% turn to peer reviews as their next verification step (G2, Apr 2026).

The flywheel works because LLMs read reviews to form opinions about brands, buyers read reviews to verify AI recommendations, and both feed the same loop. Kevin Indig's analysis of 30,000 AI citations across 500 G2 software categories found a measurable relationship: categories with 10% more reviews see roughly 2% more AI citations (Indig/G2, Jun 2026). Volume is a real lever.

G2 is the most cited B2B software platform in AI search, with 22.4% influence on software-related queries across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Mode (Indig/G2, Jun 2026). Across three software categories G2 analyzed — AEO tools, SEO tools, and marketing — the pattern was consistent: categories and products with deeper review foundations get cited more often. Star rating alone does not move the needle. Volume, recency, and category clarity do.

## The Attribution Blind Spot

Here is the uncomfortable truth for every marketing team still measuring success through MQL volume and last-click attribution: 70–80% of the B2B buyer journey now happens in untracked channels (Similarweb dark funnel analysis, 2026; Forrester/Gartner, 2026). Shortlist formation happens inside the chatbot before the buyer visits your site, so first-touch, last-touch, and multi-touch attribution only record the journey after the three vendors are already chosen.

G2's qualitative interviews with 39 B2B software marketers found widespread recognition of this gap — and widespread inability to close it. Most teams are still measuring what they have always measured: page rankings, domain authority, and click-through rates. Those signals do not tell you how ChatGPT describes your company, or whether Gemini includes you in its answers (G2, Apr 2026).

The Forrester 2026 buyer survey put the typical business buying decision at 13 internal stakeholders plus 9 external influencers (Forrester, 2026). Gartner found 61% of B2B buyers now prefer a rep-free buying experience (Gartner, Jun 2025). The buying group runs parallel research streams in channels marketing cannot see: private Slack communities, AI chats, podcasts, newsletters, YouTube, peer DMs. The dashboard that reports "marketing-sourced pipeline" by last-touch is reporting the wrong number.

## The Evaluation Maze

AI has compressed discovery but redistributed friction. G2's July 2026 Buyer Behavior Report found that evaluation is now the longest stage of the buying journey for 40% of buyers, ahead of research at 36% and decision at 22% (G2, Jul 2026). Buyers get to a shortlist faster, but the shortlist faces more internal scrutiny than ever.

The bottlenecks are specific. IT security review is the single biggest source of delay between selecting a vendor and completing a purchase, cited by 39% of buyers overall and 50% among enterprise buyers (G2, Jul 2026). Budget approval follows at 32%, implementation planning at 25%. Finance involvement in software decisions jumped from 31% to 46% in a single year (G2, Jul 2026).

AI agents are entering the evaluation process, but buyers are not handing over autonomy. 61% of buyers currently use or plan to use AI agents as part of the buying process (G2, Jul 2026). The most common use cases are evaluation tasks: understanding total cost of ownership at 51%, building shortlists at 51%, researching solutions at 49%, and evaluating shortlisted vendors at 46%. But only 9% are comfortable letting agents execute purchases within approved guardrails, and just 2% would allow purchases without pre-approval (G2, Jul 2026).

## What to Build: The Answer-Economy Playbook

The data produces a clear operational sequence. Three investments, in order.

**First, win the AI answer.** Before a human is ever involved, buyers are researching, comparing, and shortlisting with AI. Make it easy for chatbots and agents to find you, understand what you do, and recommend you. This means structured content that answers category and competitor comparison queries — the prompts two-thirds of buyers start with. It means presence on the third-party surfaces LLMs read: review platforms, community discussion, editorial comparisons. Your homepage is step two. The AI answer is step one.

**Second, build the review flywheel.** Reviews are both the fuel that feeds AI confidence and the verification layer buyers reach for when they doubt the answer. Treat review volume, recency, and category clarity as one system. Solicit reviews at every customer journey stage. Coach customers to write context-rich reviews that speak to specific problems solved and alternatives considered — the details LLMs extract and buyers trust. Being included on a G2 Best Software Awards list is a top trust signal that AI chatbots look for (G2, Apr 2026).

**Third, arm the internal champion.** By the time a buyer reaches your team, they have already shortlisted, evaluated, and often chosen. But they face a gauntlet of internal approval: security review, budget scrutiny, CFO veto, stakeholder alignment. 64% of buyers encounter AI inaccuracies frequently (G2, Apr 2026). The brands that win are the ones that make it easiest for buyers to say yes and defend that decision internally. Security documentation, ROI estimates, implementation plans, and a clear explanation of how your AI features work are not nice-to-haves. They are deal-closing infrastructure.
