<DefinitionCard>
  **PR for AI search** — earning third-party coverage, mentions and citations in the specific
  sources that AI engines retrieve from before they generate an answer. It is traditional
  earned-media strategy rebuilt around a different outcome: AI citation share. The data is
  unambiguous about why this matters — across four consecutive Muck Rack studies (July 2025
  through May 2026), earned media has held between **82% and 89% of all AI citations**, with
  paid placements at 0.3%. The machine can't interview you; it trusts what everyone else
  says about you.
</DefinitionCard>

This article turns the 2026 citation data — 25-million-link analyses, 680-million-citation
syntheses, a controlled distribution experiment — into a working PR playbook, with the
per-engine audience map that most coverage misses, and the honest caveats that most agencies
skip.

## The bias is structural, not promotional

The earned-media dominance is the most reproduced finding in the 2026 literature because
every major dataset lands on the same number:

- **Muck Rack, May 2026** ("What Is AI Reading?"): 25 million cited links across ChatGPT,
  Claude and Gemini, 17 industries → earned media 84%, paid/advertorial 0.3%, press releases
  1.1%, ~99% non-paid, journalism ~27% of all citations.
- **5WPR, May 2026**: synthesis of 680 million tracked citations across six surfaces → 85.5%
  earned; ~80% of ChatGPT's top-10 source share is encyclopedic and editorial.
- **AirOps / Superlines**: ~85% of brand mentions in AI answers originate on third-party
  pages; a brand is ~6.5x more likely to be cited through third-party sources than its own
  domain.
- **University of Toronto (arXiv 2509.08919)**: controlled experiments found AI systems
  show a "systematic and overwhelming" preference for earned media over brand-owned and
  social content — in stark contrast to Google's balanced mix.

The mechanism is the confirmation problem: an engine that cites *your* claim from *your* page
is trusting a single interested party. A claim that appears across independent editorial,
academic, government and community sources looks like a verified fact. Every "earned" item is
an independent confirmation node — the same logic that drives the
[entity-corroboration build](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026). BrightEdge puts
the boundary bluntly: ChatGPT includes brands in ~99% of eCommerce answers only when the
brand has first been resolved through sufficient independent credible coverage. No
third-party record, no citation — regardless of domain authority.

## The per-engine audience map

Earned media is not a single channel. Every engine reads a different source mix, and the
overlap is tiny — about 11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity, and in
Foglift's Q3 2026 benchmark (75 identical buyer questions × 5 engines) the mean pairwise
overlap dropped to 0.094, with 69 of 92 top-25 domains exclusive to a single engine.

| Engine | Citation behavior | Where the weight sits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ChatGPT** | Cites in 96% of responses, avg ~5 | Wikipedia dominant; trade press (TechRadar is the single most-cited tech outlet); Axios is the only journalism title in the top-3 across 13 of 17 industries; industry-trend queries cite journalism at twice the rate of how-to queries |
| **Gemini** | Cites in 82% of responses, avg ~8 | Most diversified pool; YouTube + Google-owned surfaces dominate; Reddit is its top non-Google domain; Forbes, Healthline, Business Insider lead editorial |
| **Claude** | Cites in only 55% but averages ~13 when it does | Most selective; long-form editorial and academic (PubMed Central is its #1); U.S. News, Bankrate, Consumer Reports over-index |
| **Perplexity** | Retrieval-first; the fastest-citation cycle | Reddit ≈ 46.7% of top-10 share; Wikipedia ~19.8%; review aggregators (G2, Gartner, NerdWallet, Yelp) for commercial queries; the only engine where the NYT over-indexes (~2.8x) |
| **Google AI Overviews / AI Mode** | Contributory but journalism-lightest | YouTube ~29–44% citation share; Reddit, mediums; ~88% of AI Mode citations come from outside the organic top 10 |

Two dosage corrections keep this honest. LLM Pulse's analysis of 5.3 million US citations
found only **3.26%** of all citations point to news/media outlets — the rest go to Reddit,
YouTube, retailers, review platforms, academic and government sources. "Earned media" in the
84% statistic includes academics, government, encyclopedic sites and verified community
sources, not just press. And within journalism, **service journalism wins**: "best X" and
"how to Y" outlets (TechRadar, PCMag, Mashable, Tom's Guide) are cited roughly four times as
often as the NYT by ChatGPT. Hard-news prestige is not the prize; recommendation-and-review
utility is.

## The mention threshold and the compounding loop

The authority lever has moved from the link graph to the mention graph:

- **Correlations.** Branded web mentions correlate with AI Overview visibility at r ≈ 0.66
  (Ahrefs/LumenGEO), versus ~0.22 for backlinks and ~0.18 for domain authority; YouTube
  mentions run near 0.74. Brand search volume — itself built by PR — correlates at 0.334,
  the strongest known predictor of citation likelihood in the 5WPR synthesis.
- **Thresholds.** Brands on 4+ authoritative third-party platforms are ~2.8x more likely to
  be cited by ChatGPT; the 6+ external-domain band is where cross-engine consistency lives
  ([entity article](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026), Searchless data). The top
  quartile of brands by web mentions earns 10x+ the AI citations of the next quartile (169
  vs 14 on average in a 75,000-brand analysis).
- **Distribution lifts.** The largest controlled experiment to date (Stacker, March 2026:
  87 stories, 30 brands, 2,600+ prompts, 8 platforms) found a **239% median lift** in
  citations from distributing content through third-party publishers, with 97% of distributed
  stories earning at least one citation and 64% of citations coming from the publisher's
  domain rather than the brand's. Its earlier pilot reported 325%; persistence half-life was
  ~2.1x longer.
- **Recency.** >50% of journalistic citations reference material under 11 months old;
  citation rates peak in the first month. Pages updated within ~2 months average 5.0 cites
  versus 3.9 for pages older than 2 years (SE Ranking, 2026). Earned media is a depreciating
  asset — hence the cadence, not the campaign.

## The five-move playbook

1. **Map the citation list, engine by engine.** Before outreach, run 15–20 category prompts
   ("best [category] platforms", "[category] comparison", "how to choose a [tool]") through
   ChatGPT and Perplexity and log every domain. Those are your tier-1 targets. Trade press
   beats general Tier-1 in most verticals; service journalism beats hard news.
2. **Lead with citable proprietary data.** Original research is the only content type AI
   cites regardless of domain. Semrush's 2026 PR guide makes the mechanism explicit:
   publishers link to the research page, the page acquires authority, engines cite it. Cited
   press releases contain roughly twice the statistics of non-cited ones. "Our analysis of
   500 B2B SaaS companies found X" is a citation anchor; "we're excited to announce" is not.
3. **Get into the lists AI already reuses.** Ranked roundups and comparison pages are the
   single highest-leverage content format — roughly a third of content citations are
   listicles (Ranqo), and one "best X" page can surface a brand across many answers. Review
   platforms count as separate corroboration surfaces: claiming a Trustpilot profile moved
   citation rates from ~1% to 54% in a Trustpilot/Seer study, and G2 review velocity
   correlates with citation share.
4. **Align coverage to the entity record.** Earned coverage only pays off when engines can
   attribute it. Keep name, founding date, category description and product facts identical
   across the placement, your site, Wikidata, Crunchbase and LinkedIn before you spend on
   more placements — coverage on a broken entity foundation compounds confusion, not
   citations.
5. **Measure citation outcomes, not clips.** PR's 2026 metric is AI share of voice (your
   mentions across a prompt portfolio relative to competitors), citation lift, and
   competitive citation displacement — measured monthly with a stable prompt set. Publication
   volume is a vanity metric; the [measurement buyer's guide](/resources/research/ai-visibility-measurement-buyers-guide)
   shows how to run that battery without fooling yourself.

## Read the multipliers cautiously

Two flags before you sell this program internally. First, several headline numbers come from
PR firms selling measurement (Muck Rack, 5WPR) — they cross-confirm each other tightly,
which is why we quote the range (82–89%) rather than any single point. Second, distribution
*multipliers* can be inflated by platform growth: the Watanabe natural experiment we used in
the [taxonomy article](/resources/research/seo-geo-aeo-llmo-guide-2026) found raw AEO growth
of ~6x masked a ~3.5x platform tailwind and a defensible ~2x effect. Stacker's 239% is a
controlled experiment, not a blended before/after, so it is more trustworthy — but any single
number should be validated against your own on-domain control: publish on your site alone for
a quarter, distribute for a quarter, diff the citation rates.

For the demand-growth half of this engine — why brand search volume is the strongest
citation predictor — the [brand-entity build](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026)
and the [measured program](/resources/research/ai-visibility-measurement-buyers-guide) close
the loop: recognition earns the mention, earned media multiplies it, and measurement proves
whether it moved.
