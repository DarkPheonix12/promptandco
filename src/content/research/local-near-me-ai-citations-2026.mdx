vertical where AI answers and small businesses meet most directly — and the most measurable one.
This article assembles the 2026 evidence into one operator decision framework: how big the
visibility gap is, what actually gets cited when an engine does answer a "near me" query, the
engine differences that make a single strategy wrong, and the play that fits inside the window
still open. It deliberately complements our [community piece](/resources/research/reddit-community-ai-citations-2026)

## The gap: 1.2% of businesses are AI-recommendable

Start with the number that defines the opportunity. **SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index** found
AI platforms recommend roughly **1.2% of locations on ChatGPT** and **7.4% on Perplexity** —
against **35.9%** visible in Google's local 3-pack. Meanwhile the interface that replaced most
local browsing is already everywhere: **Whitespark's 2026 study** puts AI Overviews on **68% of
local searches** versus **39%** for the traditional local pack — a 29-point gap — and higher for
the queries that actually book jobs: **92%** of informational local queries ("how long does an
eye exam take near me?") and **97%** of hybrid-intent searches ("average cost of dental implants
in Phoenix").

Consumers have moved faster than the maps. **BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey**
(n=1,002) found **45% of consumers now use ChatGPT or another generative AI tool for local
business recommendations** — up from 6% the prior year, a **7.5x increase in twelve months**. The
mismatch between that demand and the 1.2% supply is the whole thesis: AI local search is early,
measurable, and concentrated, in that order.

## What actually gets cited: the two-source system

The most instructive local-citation evidence of the year is **Steady Demand's AI Citation Ledger**
(Ben Fisher): 1,500 "best plumber near me"-style questions across 50 U.S. metros and 10
local-service categories, yielding 10,229 citations. Three findings reorganize the playbook:

- **Gemini's largest citation source is the business's own website — about 50%** of all
  citations (49.6% in the September refresh, down from 58.8% in July). That is more than every
  directory, forum, and review platform combined, though the margin narrowed from ~22 points to
  ~7 as directories absorbed lost community share. A thin generic website is still a real
  disadvantage; on-site content is doing retrieval work, not being bypassed.
- **ChatGPT reads a different part of the web.** On identical queries it cites the business's own
  site just **10.5%** of the time and leans on **general business directories at 46.0%** (versus
  17.7% for Gemini). Ask both engines the same question and they overlap on only **8.3%** of
  cited domains and name the same top business **4.9%** of the time.
- **The long tail belongs to individual businesses.** Of the 2,474 unique domains cited, most are
  individual companies appearing once or twice; Gemini named 3,738 distinct businesses, only
  1.8% of which were recommended in more than one metro (those were nationals like Terminix or
  Orkin). Citation share in AI answers is not consolidated the way Google's first page has become.

The directories never stopped mattering — they just became the floor everywhere. Angi,
HomeAdvisor, BBB and ConsumerAffairs appear in all 50 metros Steady Demand collected; a second
tier (Thumbtack, Expertise.com) appears in most. When Reddit collapsed out of both engines between
July and September 2026 — 13.7% to 1.7% of Gemini's citations in five weeks, ChatGPT earlier,
with no announcement from either — the directories absorbed almost all of it (19.1% Gemini).
Community presence is no longer a local citation channel; directories and your own site are.

## How LLMs rank local: semantic relevance, not authority

**SearchAtlas's empirical study** (104,855 URL citations from OpenAI, Gemini, Perplexity, Grok,
Google AI Mode and Copilot, Oct 27–Dec 3 2025, merged with Google Business Profile data on 14,300
citations) is the cleanest test of local ranking-in-AI yet run. Three results carry most of the
weight:

- **Classical authority is decoupled.** Domain Authority, Domain Power and Domain Rating showed
  *negative* correlations with LLM citation position across most platforms — strongest for
  OpenAI, Grok and AI Mode. A higher-authority domain does not reliably earn a better citation
  slot in a "near me" answer.
- **Semantic relevance is the primary signal.** Correlation with citation rank ran ~0.18–0.19 for
  OpenAI, Grok and Google AI Mode, ~0.10 for Perplexity, ~0.04 for Copilot. The engine that
  ranks your citations first is the one whose content best matches query intent.
- **Google Business Profile signals are modest but real.** GBP composite score was the strongest
  business signal (~+0.16); replied reviews (+0.11) and review count (+0.10) beat raw rating
  (+0.06); posts and citation count were near-zero; map heatmap average rank was *negative*.
  GBP is supporting context for AI citation, not the key. All effect sizes were small
  (≈−0.01…+0.16).

Freshness is real but platform-specific: Gemini is the strongest recency-preferring engine
(~+0.13), OpenAI moderate, while Copilot, Grok and AI Mode favor evergreen pages. And the
mechanism underneath all of it matches the audit evidence: **DeepDiveAudits' 47-site audit** (GTA
home-services, Jul 2025–Apr 2026) found 88% of sites fail at least four of six signals — schema
81%, original data 96%, author identity 94%, direct-answer lead 91%, entity density 73% — with
retested sites that fixed all six earning an AI Overview citation within 6–9 weeks. The AI
Overview is a retrieval-and-composition layer on the same index with stricter filters
(Speakable-grade lead answers, authored content, original data), not a separate ranking system.
Roughly 80% of top-1-ranking pages in classic search are AI-relevant by proxy — 25,000-query
analyses put the #1 result inside an AI Overview about 25% of the time — but map rank is not the
mechanism.

## The honesty section: volatility and the AI-vs-packs instability

Three recent facts belong in every local strategy deck, because each kills a stale assumption:

- **The packs are stable; AI answers are not.** Running the identical repeated query against
  Google's own local pack returns the same top listing **83.3%** of the time — versus **5.1%**
  for Gemini's generated recommendation (Steady Demand). AI instability is a feature of the
  format; plan for change, don't chase it.
- **Engines disagree, structurally.** Gemini vs ChatGPT local citation-overlap: 8.3%; same top
  business: 4.9%. This echoes our [five-engine divergence thread](/resources/research/ai-mode-vs-ai-overviews-2026):
  divergence is at query level, convergence at ecosystem level.
- **Schema is not a switch.** Ahrefs tracked 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD between August 2025
  and March 2026 and found **no measurable 30-day citation change** from AI Overviews, AI Mode or
  ChatGPT — and five engines extract only visible HTML on a live fetch. The correlational "schema
  + FAQ = +44% citations" figures floating around are real but panel-level; treat marked-up
  answer-first content as correct hygiene, not a toggle.

## The play: what fits inside the window

Local SEO is not dead; it now runs on two engines. The operator sequence, priced for the 12–18
months before the market converges:

1. **Fix the on-site answer layer first.** Answer-first service pages: a direct 60–100 word answer
   to the money question above the fold (Kevin Indig's 1.2-million-response analysis puts 44.2%
   of citations in the first 30% of a page), entity-rich and definitive, with local FAQ and a
   pricing/cost guide — **price/cost/buy queries trigger AI Overviews more than 80% of the time**
   and most local businesses leave them unanswered.
2. **Publish original market data.** Local benchmarks (seasonal cost ranges, response-time
   averages, permit steps) are citation-magnets for the same reason any unique data is: an AI
   that references the fact must reference the source.
3. **Make the entity legible everywhere.** NAP, categories, hours and service area identical
   across GBP, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, BBB and the directories you claim — a quarterly
   audit. 67% of consumers do not fact-check AI sources (GatherUp), so inconsistency does
   double damage.
4. **Run the review cadence, not just the count.** 47% of consumers won't use a business under
   20 reviews; 31% require 4.5 stars or higher; 19% expect a same-day reply (up from 6%). Replied
   reviews are also the second-strongest GBP signal in the SearchAtlas data (+0.11).
5. **Claim the directory floor.** In an engine where directories are 46% of citations (ChatGPT),
   your listings are your supply. Angi, HomeAdvisor, Expertise, Birdeye, BBB and Thumbtack
   absorbed the entire Reddit collapse — this is the part of the strategy you can actually buy
   and verify.

## Bottom line

AI local search in 2026 is an answer economy with thin supply: **1.2% of businesses recommendable
on ChatGPT vs 35.9% in the 3-pack**, against 45% of consumers already asking — and a 68%-vs-39%
screen gap where AI answers have replaced the packs for informational and hybrid queries. The
evidence says the mechanism is semantic relevance, not authority; that Gemini and ChatGPT read
different parts of the local web (own-site ~50% vs ~10.5%); that **GBP and reviews are supporting
context, not switches**; and that directories are the universal floor. The window is real and
labeled: **12–18 months** while ~20% of organizations have actually started AEO, and the engines
are still building their trusted local source sets. Fix the on-site answer layer, publish original
local data, keep the entity consistent, run the review cadence, claim the directory floor — and
treat every one of these numbers as a dated snapshot, because local AI citation share moves in
weeks, not years. Continue from the [11-levers hub](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026),
and for the review-signal mechanics behind this play, see the E-E-A-T and trust-signals article.
