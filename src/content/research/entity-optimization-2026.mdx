  **Entity optimization** — making your brand resolvable as one verified thing across every
  surface an AI engine reads: your site, your schema, Wikidata, LinkedIn, Crunchbase,
  review platforms, and press coverage. The mechanism is structural, not stylistic: AI
  engines run an entity-resolution pass *before* selecting sources, so a brand an engine
  cannot confidently resolve never reaches the stage where citation is decided.

In the [11-levers model](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026), levers 9 and 10 are
about authority and earned corroboration. This article builds the layer underneath them.
The cleanest way to see it is a controlled experiment run in April 2026: the GEO Lab tested
a single domain across 22 competition queries at entity densities ranging from 12.9 to 29.5
entities per thousand words — and got **zero citations at every density level**, because
the test domain had never cleared entity recognition in the first place (Ferreira, 2026,
preprint on Zenodo). Page-level signal did nothing until the machine could answer one
question first: *who published this, and do independent sources agree?*

## The mechanism: resolution before citation

Every answer surface — Google's Knowledge Graph and Gemini, ChatGPT's retrieval layer,
Perplexity's index, Brave/Claude's live fetches — resolves the entities in a query before
it collects sources. The practical consequence is a decision hierarchy with three layers:

1. **Recognition** — does the system know your brand exists and what it is?
2. **Disambiguation** — when multiple near-identical names exist, does it pick *you*?
3. **Corroboration** — do independent sources agree with the facts you assert?

An engine that cannot clear the first two layers will still use your content — it will just
attribute it to nobody, or to a competitor it *can* resolve. Growth Memo's 2026 analysis
named this the **ghost citation**: content informing an answer while the brand receives no
credit, because the entity behind the page is unresolved (Indig, 2026). If you have ever
felt "we get quoted but never named" in an AI answer, this is the mechanism.

## The 2026 evidence that entity work moves the needle

- **Recognition gating is measurable.** In a 2026 tracking study of 500+ brands across
  ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini, **88% had no structured entity data at all**, 42% were
  not mentioned by ChatGPT in category questions even with real organic presence, 61% were
  cited inconsistently across engines — and only 2.4% appeared consistently on all three,
  every one of which had entity signals across six or more external domains (Searchless.ai,
  2026). Recognition, not content volume, segregated the visible from the invisible.
- **Entity-representation feeds the largest brand-name dataset.** Google's curated
  Knowledge Graph and its Wikidata-backed structured records are what Gemini's
  entity-verification layer draws on when deciding whether to *name* a source rather than
  borrow from it anonymously (llmreach, 2026). Brands without a high-confidence record get
  content used, then remain unnamed — the ghost-citation state.
- **Mention density behaves like a floor.** In a July 2026 test of 175 brands, 96% were
  described accurately when asked about *directly*, yet 89% never appeared in answers when
  not named — and brands with fewer than ~2,000 indexed web pages mentioning them were
  named in answers just **3% of the time** (Victorious, 2026). Corroboration volume acts as
  a gate below which naming rarely happens.
- **Given recognition, page quality decides the slot.** In the same GEO Lab that found
  zero citations on an unrecognized domain, queries about concepts that domain itself
  originated produced ~20% citation once recognition cleared (Ferreira, 2026). That is the
  exact split this article is about: entity work buys you admission; evidence-rich content
  (levers 5–8 in the [hub article](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026)) wins the slot.

## The four-part build

**1. Give the entity a home page.** One canonical URL — almost always your About page —
that functions as the source of truth: an Organization JSON-LD block with `@id` set to
your canonical domain plus founding date, leadership, category, and a one-line description
(standardized practice; Kalicube's formalization of the entity home, March 2026). Google
now pulls Knowledge Panel descriptions from this page when a Wikipedia article is absent.
State who you are in plain prose too — engines resolve what the text says, not just what
the markup claims.

**2. Establish the machine-readable identity: Wikidata QID.** Wikidata applies no
notability threshold and feeds the Knowledge Graph as structured data. Create the item —
instance of (P31), official website (P856), founding date (P571), one-line description — and
record the QID. Support-data research in 2026 found Wikipedia citation volume correlating
strongly with LLM recommendation visibility in category questions (rho ≈ 0.58 in CRM
software; Wills, 2026); Wikidata is the achievable half of that signal for brands that will
not meet Wikipedia's bar.

**3. Wire the sameAs array and enforce identity consistency.** Your Organization schema's
`sameAs` array is a merge instruction: each URL tells the engine that profile describes the
same entity as your canonical domain. Point it at Wikidata, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, your
Google Business Profile, GitHub and verified social profiles — then make every profile
agree, *verbatim*, on the canonical name and the one-line description. Declare genuine name
variants in `alternateName` rather than letting engines guess. This is NAP consistency
raised from "where you are" to "what you are": each consistent profile is independent
confirmation, each drift splits your corroboration across thin fragments.

**4. Earn cross-domain corroboration.** Recognition is ultimately confirmed by *other
people's* pages. Research on "entity chains" frames each earned placement — a feature, an
analyst mention, a review, a podcast, a profile — as a verification node on an independent
domain engines already retrieve, with five or more independent domains carrying consistently
described facts as the commonly observed working threshold (Machine Relations, 2026). The
signal-shape data matches: branded *mentions* correlate with AI visibility roughly three
times more strongly than backlinks (≈0.66 vs ≈0.22; Ahrefs, 2025-26), and brand-name
presence on review and profile platforms materially raises ChatGPT citation probability
(Link Building Journal synthesis, 2026). Every unlinked mention still counts toward the
entity layer, which is why digital PR has become entity infrastructure.

## The 30-day entity sprint

- **Week 1:** audit state — does a Knowledge Panel exist, is a Wikidata QID present, does
  your About page carry a complete Organization block, which profiles disagree? Use the
  free Knowledge Graph Search API and brand-name searches in ChatGPT and Gemini ("What is
  [brand]? What does it do and where is it based?") and log every error.
- **Week 2:** rewrite the About page as an entity home; ship Organization JSON-LD with
  `@id`, `name`, `url`, `foundingDate`, `description`, `logo`.
- **Week 3:** create the Wikidata item; record the QID; build the `sameAs` array including
  it; align LinkedIn, Crunchbase, Google Business Profile and directories to the one
  canonical name and description.
- **Week 4 onward:** re-run the brand prompts monthly and the Knowledge Graph API check
  quarterly. The deltas between "what the engine says now" and "what the engine said last
  month" are your progress report — the errors that persist point at the source that still
  disagrees.

Timeline honesty: the identity layer registers within weeks; corroboration compounds over
6–12 months; recognition in crowded categories typically takes a quarter or more
(Foragentis, 2026; Jottler, 2026). It is a slow, compounding signal. Nothing in this
article happens overnight, and nothing about it replaces the content and
[technical](/resources/research/ai-crawlers-2026-readiness) layers — it unlocks them.

## What must still be handled separately

Entity work is necessary, not sufficient. Three gaps in the 2026 data keep it honest:

- **Entity density is not content quality.** The GEO Lab's controlled test found density
  alone did nothing before recognition. Don't stuff entity names into prose; build the
  record, then win slots with evidence (the 
  [11-levers hub](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026) is the content half).
- **Per-engine layers have diverged.** ChatGPT's source layer rebalanced hard in late 2025
  (Reddit citations ~60% → ~10%, Wikipedia ~55% → under 20%; Semrush, 230,000 prompts,
  November 2025), Google AI Mode cites Wikipedia in only ~2% of responses, and LinkedIn
  holds at roughly 15% as the most stable source. One identity, several engine layers:
  the same QID and sameAs spine serve all of them, which is exactly why a single entity
  foundation out-earns engine-specific reshuffling.
- **The Knowledge Panel is a lagging indicator.** Its absence does not mean failure, and
  its presence is not the prize. What you actually want is naming behavior: use the
  brand-prompt polling in the sprint above to test whether engines *name* you rather than
  merely describe your category.

For the measurable side of this — does your share of named mentions move — the
[citation-intelligence buyer's guide](/resources/research/ai-visibility-measurement-buyers-guide)
covers what to track and how to avoid fooling yourself with a noisy week.
