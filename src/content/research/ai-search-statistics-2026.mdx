This is the statistics page for Prompt&Co.'s research hub. It aggregates the published 2026 AI-search
data we reference across our service pages, industry playbooks and guides — with sources, dates and,
where the numbers disagree, an explanation of why.

The point of this page is not to drown you in numbers. It is to give you the handful of statistics
that actually bear on AI visibility decisions, and to separate the findings that replicate from the
headline numbers that don't.

## The headline numbers

These are the statistics most worth knowing — the ones that show up in strategy conversations and that
bear on what GEO, AEO and AI visibility work actually involve.

### AI is present in roughly half of US searches

**BrightEdge (early 2026):** AI Overview presence around **48%** of US queries, with wide vertical
variance — roughly **88%** for healthcare, **83%** for education, **82%** for B2B technology, and
low single digits for some retail query patterns.

**SparkToro (2026):** roughly **68%** of US searches end without a click — the zero-click baseline
that AI Overviews sit on top of.

**Ahrefs (2026):** roughly **58%** CTR reduction for top results when AI Overviews appear on the query.

The takeaway: AI is now a normal part of the search layer for a large share of queries, and its
presence suppresses clicks to the traditional results. The brands that only optimize for clicks are
optimizing for a shrinking slice of the discovery pipeline.

### Most AI citations are one-and-done

**Trakkr (2026):** across **108K URLs, 8 models and 10 months**, **73.5%** of cited URLs appeared
exactly once. Brand-level citation presence decays with a **half-life around 31 days**.

**Wellows (2026):** across **22.7M citations**, **79.6%** of sources were cited by exactly one engine;
just **0.31%** were cited by all five engines studied.

**SurfacedBy (2026):** **127,198 citations across 5 engines including Claude**; the "single-engine-only"
figure in a comparable sample was **69.6%**.

The takeaway: citation presence is volatile, not cumulative. A single citation snapshot is not a trend,
and citation equity does not compound the way backlinks were taught to. Measurement cadence matters.

### Only a minority of brand mentions become citations

**BuzzStream (June 2026):** across **12,000 AI responses**, only about **23%** of brand mentions became
citations — meaning being named in an answer is necessary but not sufficient to be a cited source.

The takeaway: mention equity and citation equity are different. A brand can be named in an answer without
being a cited source, and the gap between the two is where a lot of visibility work sits.

### Ranking and citation overlap is real but modest

**Ahrefs (March 2026):** ~**37.9%** of AI Overview-cited URLs also rank in Google's top 10 — across
**4M cited URLs and 863K keywords**.

**seoClarity (October 2025):** ~**32%** of top sources for cited keywords rank in the top 10 — across
**362K keywords**.

**The Stacc (May 2026):** **38%** of cited pages rank in the top 10 — across **1,000 AI Overviews**.

**DerivateX (July 2026):** ~**35%** cross-engine source overlap for B2B queries — across **100 queries
and 1,259 citations**.

**BrightEdge (longitudinal):** AI Overviews' overlap with the organic top 10 rose from roughly **32%**
in May 2024 to roughly **54.5%** by late 2025.

**5WPR (May 2026):** AI answer vs ranking overlap for branded queries fell from roughly **70%** to
under **20%** over about two years of tracked branded queries.

The takeaway: the overlap is real, it is moderate, and it is not the same number everywhere. Ranking
helps you get cited — it is not the same as being cited. The longitudinal disagreement (BrightEdge's rise
vs 5WPR's collapse) is method: AI Overviews increasingly mirror Google's own top results, while standalone
assistants such as ChatGPT build their own source pools.

### The top 1% of domains collect a disproportionate share of citations

**The Stacc (May 2026):** across **1,000 AI Overviews**, the top **1%** of domains collected roughly
**47%** of citations. Health vertical analyses show similar concentration — top-10 domains ≈ **71%** of
citations, led by NIH, Mayo Clinic and CDC.

The takeaway: citation authority is concentrated. If you are not in the elite for your category, your
fight is real but winnable — the other ~53% is a long tail of thousands of mid-sized domains.

### Source types vary sharply by engine

**Google AI Overviews (1,000-AI-Overview sample, The Stacc):** Wikipedia ~**24%**, Reddit ~**22%**,
YouTube ~**23%** of cited domains.

**ChatGPT (Semrush 100M-citation analysis, 2026):** Reddit share collapsed from ~**60%** to ~**10%**
over the study period; the engine rotates UGC and product pages.

**Claude (five-engine study, SurfacedBy):** ~**0.02%** of citations went to YouTube — far below the
20%+ typical of other engines. Claude favors documentation and primary sources.

**Perplexity:** the most link-transparent assistant; citation density high and stable.

**Gemini:** cites in only ~**41%** of answers (cloro, SurfacedBy, OtterlyAI); when it does cite, it is
source-dense.

The takeaway: there is no single "make yourself citable" playbook. The right playbook is engine-aware —
what works for ChatGPT visibility is not identical to what works for Gemini or Claude.

### Controlled experiments find specific levers that move visibility

**Aggarwal et al. (KDD 2024), plus published replications:** in controlled GEO experiments,

- **Named sources (expert quotes + attribution):** ~**+41%** visibility
- **Statistics and data in answers:** ~**+33%** visibility
- **Citations and references in content:** ~**+28%** visibility
- **FAQ structured data:** ~**+44%** visibility in the experiment
- **Keyword stuffing:** ~**-8%** visibility (a negative control)

**The Stacc (1,000 AI Overviews):** FAQ and how-to schema pages cited at roughly **2.3–2.8×** the
baseline rate of pages without.

**Freshness (large citation sample):** content updated within roughly the last month cited at
~**3.2×** the rate of older content.

The takeaway: the levers with the best evidence are structural and substantive — named sources,
data, citations, FAQ schema, freshness — and the one clear negative is keyword stuffing. These are
evidence-backed, not guaranteed, and they compound best as part of a broader program.

### Technical barriers are common and consequential

**OtterlyAI (February 2026):** across a **1M-citation sample**, roughly **73%** of sites carried at
least one technical barrier that could prevent citation — primarily client-side rendering that hides
content from crawlers.

The takeaway: if an AI agent cannot parse your page's raw HTML, nothing else you do matters. This is
the highest-impact, least-glamorous GEO fix, and it is prerequisite to everything else.

## The statistics by category

### B2B and SaaS

**G2 Buyer Behavior (2026):** **82%** of B2B buyers sourced recommendations from an AI chatbot in
the last 24 months; AI chatbots were the **#1** influence on the vendor shortlist, ahead of review
sites; **69%** of buyers say AI research surfaced information that changed their vendor choice.

**G2 (2026):** B2B software buyers who start research with an AI chatbot more often than with Google:
**51%** (up from lower figures seven months earlier); **71%** rely on AI chatbots during software
research.

**6sense (via G2):** **80%** of deals are won by the pre-contact favorite — the vendor who was
preferred before sales ever engaged.

The takeaway: the B2B shortlist is being assembled before first contact, and AI chatbots are the
leading influence on it. For B2B brands, AI visibility is not a side channel — it is increasingly the
first gate.

### E-commerce and product discovery

**SparkToro (2026):** **68%** of US searches end without a click — product research included.

**Ahrefs (2026):** ~**58%** CTR reduction for top results when Google AI Overviews appear.

**OtterlyAI (2026):** **31.8%** of social/video citations in AI answers come from YouTube — where
product content lives.

**SparkToro (2026):** **20%+** of SERPs now show AI Overviews, with coverage still expanding.

The takeaway: product discovery is being rebuilt around answers, and video plus structured product data
are underweighted by most e-commerce strategies relative to their citation weight.

### Local search

**SearchAtlas (104,855 local citations, 2026):** **+0.16** correlation between Google Business Profile
signals and AI local ranking — the strongest measured factor; **+0.11** for businesses that reply to
reviews.

**Cairrot (1.5M-prompt study, 2026):** **1.83→3.17%** local "near me" lift on Perplexity when local
signals are present.

**5W (680M-citation audit, 2026):** ~**25%** of US ChatGPT citations draw on Wikipedia + Reddit
combined.

The takeaway: for local businesses, profiles and review behavior — not domain authority — are the
measured drivers of AI local visibility. Domain authority shows a *negative* correlation in local AI
results in the SearchAtlas data.

### Trust-heavy professional categories

**Red-engage (10k-citation study, 2026):** **46.7%** of Perplexity's citations flow through Reddit-style
community discussion.

**5W (680M-citation audit, 2026):** ~**25%** of US ChatGPT citations draw on Wikipedia + Reddit
combined.

**Overthink Group (2026):** **70.8%** of citations for "best/top" commercial queries come from
listicle-style pages.

**G2 (2026):** **38%** of B2B buyers say review sites are their most confidence-inspiring shortlist
signal.

The takeaway: for trust-heavy categories, third-party corroboration — community discussion, listicles,
review platforms — dominates the sources AI systems cite. A brand's own website is necessary but not
sufficient to be recommended.

## The method note on conflicting numbers

Several of the statistics above appear to conflict — BrightEdge's rising overlap vs 5WPR's collapsing
overlap, for example. The reconciliation is method, not error:

- **Different engines:** AI Overviews increasingly mirrors Google's own top results (BrightEdge's rising
  line), while standalone assistants such as ChatGPT build their own source pools (5WPR's falling line).
- **Different query sets:** branded vs non-branded, commercial vs informational — the overlap behaves
  differently for each.
- **Different definitions of "overlap":** "cited pages that rank" vs "ranking pages that get cited" are
  not the same number.

When you see a single AI-search statistic, the right question is not "is it true?" but "what exactly is
it measuring, on what query set, for which engine?" The numbers above are reported as published, with
the method where it matters.
