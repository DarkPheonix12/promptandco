We are not giving you a numbered ranking. The "best" GEO agency for a B2B SaaS company in the US is
not the best for a local-services business in the UK, and a ranked list implies a precision the category

## What to actually look for when evaluating

If you are comparing agencies for a GEO or AI-search engagement, these are the questions that separate
real work from rebranded SEO:

1. **Baseline before pitch.** Does the agency measure your current AI visibility across a defined prompt
   panel before selling you a program — and can they show you the method? If they lead with a price before
   a baseline, that is a package, not a program.

2. **Engine-specific, not generic.** Do they talk about specific engines, source types, citation patterns
   and category dynamics — or do they use "AI search" as a synonym for everything? The category is
   engine-aware; a generic pitch is a generic agency.

3. **Proof you can inspect.** Do they have real client proof with verifiable numbers, or placeholder case
   studies with invented percentages? If their proof is "SaaS Company Increases AI Mentions by 340%" with
   no named client, no evidence, and no way to check — that is a pattern, not an outlier.

4. **Measurement as a product, not a slide.** Do they treat measurement as a core deliverable — ongoing
   prompt-panel monitoring with share of answer, citation sources, sentiment and accuracy — or as a
   one-time audit slide? The evidence says citation presence decays on a ~31-day half-life. A program
   that measures once is not measuring the thing it claims to optimize.

5. **Pricing honesty.** Do they distinguish market pricing ranges from their own rates, and do they scope
   before quoting? If they lead with a fixed tier before an audit — especially at a price that would not
   cover multi-engine measurement plus content plus citation work — that is a sign.

6. **The guarantee test.** Does the agency promise guaranteed ChatGPT placement, #1 AI rankings, or
   specific citation outcomes? If yes, walk away. No one can guarantee what a third-party model generates,
   and the agencies that promise they can are the ones you should not trust with anything.

## The red flags that mean "rebranded SEO"

The specific patterns that suggest an agency is selling SEO with a GEO label:

- Fixed-price GEO tiers before any audit or baseline.
- "Guaranteed ChatGPT rankings" or "#1 in AI search" promises.
- Case studies with invented percentages and no named clients.
- No mention of specific engines, prompt panels, citation sources, or measurement cadence.
- A GEO page that is a thin rehash of the agency's SEO page with the word "AI" inserted.
- No published content that demonstrates GEO-specific understanding beyond the marketing vocabulary.
- Resistance to showing the method behind their measurement.

One red flag alone may be a communication issue. Several together are a pattern.
