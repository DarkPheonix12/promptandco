By September 2026, the question "who does AI cite?" has moved from speculation to
measurement. A wave of public datasets spanning millions of AI answers makes it possible to
see how ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, AI Mode and Copilot behave as
citers. The findings are useful and sometimes counterintuitive. But the numbers also
disagree with each other, sometimes sharply — and the difference is method, not error.

This is the edition we can publish honestly today: a source-by-source synthesis of that
research, an explanation of why the headline numbers conflict, a per-engine read of
citation behavior, and a playbook of the levers that have evidence behind them. We also lay
out the original panel we are building, because as of today no public dataset covers all six
engines with an open, replicable method.

## The headlines worth knowing

**AI is present in roughly half of US searches.** BrightEdge's tracking put AI Overviews
presence around 48% of queries in early 2026, with wide variance by vertical — roughly 88%
for healthcare, 83% for education, 82% for B2B technology, and low single digits for some
retail query patterns. Different trackers use different methods, so treat any single number
as a range, not a fact.

**Most citations are one-and-done.** The most robust finding in the field is that citation
pools are shallow and volatile. Across ten months and eight models, Trakkr found 73.5% of
cited URLs appeared only once, with brand-level citation presence decaying at a half-life of
about 31 days. In parallel, Wellows reported 79.6% of sources cited by exactly one engine
(just 0.31% cited by all five), and SurfacedBy put the "single engine only" figure at 69.6%.

**Gemini is the outlier citer.** Across the cloro, SurfacedBy and OtterlyAI datasets, Gemini
cites sources in only about 41% of answers while the other engines cite 95–98% of the time.
If your brand is invisible in Gemini answers, that may be a symptom of Gemini's lower
citation rate rather than of you.

**Ranking and being cited are different jobs.** The overlap between "ranks in Google's top
10" and "gets cited by AI" consistently lands around 32–38% across the largest datasets
(Ahrefs 37.9%, seoClarity ~32%, DerivateX 35%), while the reverse — cited pages that rank —
is much lower. Rank helps; it is not the same game.

**Freshness and structure compound.** Content updated within roughly the last month is cited
at materially higher rates across large citation samples, and pages using how-to or FAQ
structured data are cited at multiples of pages without it. Both are good news, because both
are under your control.

## Why the studies disagree (and how to read them)

The most confusing thing about AI-search coverage is that the "overlap" story points in
opposite directions at the same time. Three statements are all true:

- **5WPR tracked a collapse**: overlap between AI answers and traditional top-10 rankings
  fell from roughly 70% to under 20% across about two years of tracked branded queries.
- **BrightEdge tracked a rise**: AI Overviews' overlap with the organic top 10 rose from
  around 32% in May 2024 to roughly 54.5% by late 2025.
- **Ahrefs, seoClarity, DerivateX and The Stacc measured a stable middle**: about 32–38% of
  AI-cited URLs also rank in the organic top 10.

When studies contradict each other, the answer is method:

| Study | What "overlap" means | Scale | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ahrefs (2026-03) | % of AI-cited URLs that also rank top 10 | 4M cited URLs, 863K keywords | ~37.9% |
| seoClarity (2025-10) | % of top sources for cited keywords that rank top 10 | 362K keywords | ~32% |
| DerivateX (2026-07) | Cross-engine source overlap, B2B queries | 100 queries, 1,259 citations | ~35% |
| The Stacc (2026-05) | % of cited pages ranking in top 10 | 1,000 AI Overviews | 38% |
| BrightEdge (2025-09) | AIO vs organic top-10 overlap trend | Longitudinal | 32% → ~54.5% |
| 5WPR (2026-05) | AI answer vs ranking overlap, branded queries | ~2 years tracked | ~70% → &lt;20% |

The reconciliation matters more than any single number:

- **"Cited pages that rank"** — a URL already ranking in Google's top 10 that then gets
  cited — is what most studies measure, and it is modest (~32–38%) and drifting.
- **"Ranking pages that get cited"** is far lower; position helps but is no guarantee.
- **"Collapse vs rise"** is partly a surface difference. AI Overviews increasingly mirrors
  Google's own top results (BrightEdge's rising line), while standalone assistants such as
  ChatGPT build their own pools (5WPR's falling line). Neither study is wrong; they are
  tracking different engines through different phases.

The practical takeaway: treat every single overlap number with suspicion, and never bet a
strategy on one engine's snapshot.

## How each engine cites (per-engine signatures)

Across the 2026 datasets, the engines display distinct, reproducible citation personalities:

| Engine | Sources per answer (typical) | Citation rate | Distinctive behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT (Search) | ~3.7–12 depending on study | Cites in 95%+ of answers | Large unique source pool (~76% of its citations are unique to it in one large sample); rotates UGC and community pages heavily. |
| Google AI Mode | ~17–20 | High | Source-dense; leans on top domains (~58% from top-10 domains in one instrumentation study); shares only ~13.7% of URLs with classic AI Overviews. |
| Google AI Overviews | ~4.2 | High | Listicle-friendly; Wikipedia ~24%, Reddit ~22%, YouTube ~23% of cited domains in one 1,000-AIO sample; about 47% of citations go to the top 1% of domains. |
| Perplexity | High | 95%+ | Most link-transparent assistant; citation density high and stable. |
| Copilot | ~5 | High | Sparser citations, fewer sources per answer. |
| Gemini | ~11 (SurfacedBy) | **~41%** | The outlier: cites in well under half of answers; when it does cite, it is source-dense. |
| Claude | — | High | The outlier in source type: it largely avoids UGC — about 0.02% of Claude's citations went to YouTube in a five-engine study, far below the 20%+ typical of other engines. Prefers documentation and primary sources. |

Two structural patterns stand out.

**Assistants recycle a small elite, then fan out.** The Stacc found the top 1% of domains
collected roughly 47% of citations across 1,000 AI Overviews; health vertical analyses show
similar concentration (top-10 domains ≈ 71% of citations, led by NIH, Mayo Clinic and CDC).
If you are not in the elite for your category, your fight is real but winnable — the other
~53% is a long tail of thousands of mid-sized domains.

**The engines diverge on where they look.** The source-type signature differs sharply: AI
Overviews lean on encyclopedic and community domains (Wikipedia, Reddit, YouTube); ChatGPT
rotates UGC and product pages while its Reddit share collapsed from ~60% to ~10% in the
100M-citation sample Semrush analyzed; and Claude filters out UGC almost entirely in favor of
primary documentation. A single "make yourself citable" playbook is the wrong answer; the
right playbook is engine-aware.

## What the evidence says is under your control

The strongest, most replicable levers come from controlled experiments and replications
(Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024, plus published replications) and large citation datasets:

| Lever | Evidence | Source type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Named sources (expert quotes + attribution) | ~+41% visibility in controlled GEO tests | Controlled experiment |
| Statistics and data in answers | ~+33% visibility | Controlled experiment |
| Citations and references in content | ~+28% visibility | Controlled experiment |
| FAQ structured data | ~+44% in experiment; FAQ/how-to schema cited at ~2.3–2.8× baseline in a 1,000-AIO sample | Experiment + correlation |
| Freshness (updated within ~30 days) | ~3.2× citation rate in a large citation sample | Correlation |
| Brand mentions → citations | Only ~23% of brand mentions become citations — being named is necessary but not sufficient | Point-in-time dataset |

And the negative controls: in the same experiments, keyword stuffing actively reduced
visibility (about −8%). Google has stated it gives no special ranking weight to `llms.txt`.
And promises to "get you into ChatGPT" or guaranteed citations have no basis in the technical
literature.

Underneath everything is accessibility. When OtterlyAI examined a million-citation sample,
roughly 73% of sites carried at least one technical barrier that could prevent citation —
primarily client-side rendering that hides content from crawlers. If an AI agent cannot parse
your page's raw HTML, nothing else you do matters.

## Why you should check the engine, not default to Google

One underreported pattern: for commercial and review-style queries, AI answers are heavily
populated by third-party listicles and comparison content. DerivateX found that roughly 63%
of the AI-cited sources in its B2B topic set were exactly that kind of content.

The implications are awkward but actionable:

- **Own the source pattern.** If listicles and comparisons win, you cannot just build pages —
  you need your brand named inside the formats AI cites from, on authoritative domains.
- **Build cadence, not one-shot hits.** Trakkr's ~31-day half-life and 5WPR's 3–5 day
  citation-pool churn say: measure, then move, then measure again. Citation equity does not
  compound the way backlinks were taught to.
- **Don't measure once.** The most replicable methodology advice (Schulte and colleagues,
  2026) is blunt: run each query 7–8 or more times per session, remeasure on 2–4 week
  windows, and expect 32–43% source overlap between runs of the same prompt. Your KPI is a
  distribution, not a point.
