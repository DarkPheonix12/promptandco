  **The four terms are not four jobs.** SEO optimizes pages for rankings in a ranked list. AEO
  optimizes content to become the direct answer. GEO optimizes how content survives an
  AI pipeline and gets cited inside generated answers. LLMO optimizes how a brand appears
  in a model's own knowledge. They sit on top of each other — same infrastructure,
  different surfaces, different metrics. The 2026 evidence is that the *labels* matter less
  than most of the industry's keyboard (and every vendor) lets on.

The alphabet soup — SEO, AEO, GEO, LLMO, plus AIO and AAO — is the byproduct of a
discipline relabeling itself in real time. Search Counsel's January 2026 review frames them
as "four labels the industry coined at different moments to describe the same direction of
travel," and Serpstat's July 2026 comparison is more blunt: "the industry does not use GEO,
AEO, and LLMO consistently." This guide takes the taxonomy seriously because *which term you
adopt changes what you optimize for* — then gives you a measurement rule so the label stays
irrelevant.

## What each term actually optimizes

| Term | Coined | Surface | Unit | Goal | Signals | Typical metric |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SEO | mid-1990s | Ranked search results (Google, Bing) | Whole pages | Rank and clicks | Crawlability, relevance, links, authority | Position, CTR, organic traffic |
| AEO | ~2019, featured-snippet era; revived for AI | Direct-answer boxes, voice, AI Overviews | Concise answer blocks / pages | Selected as the answer | Definition-first structure, schema, scoped claims | Snippet share, answer capture, share of answer |
| GEO | 2024 KDD paper (Aggarwal et al.) | Generated answers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews) | Sections | Retrieved and cited | Machine extractability, evidence density, entity clarity, third-party source graph | Citation frequency, mention share |
| LLMO | emerging 2025, no canonical paper | The model layer (GPT/Claude/Gemini families) | The model's representation | Accurate, consistent brand representation | Entity consistency, authoritative coverage, training-data presence | Prompt recall rate, entity coverage, brand accuracy |

Every row above matters most for what the even the vendors keep quiet: there is no settled,
universal taxonomy. MV3's July 2026 review, Eastbound's May 2026 disambiguation and
appwebseo's August 2026 decision guide each publish mutually incompatible glossaries — which
is exactly the point: the *terms are operational shorthand*, and the work underneath is one
discovery pipeline.

## The three mental models behind the alphabet

The sharpest framework comes from the GEO Lab (Ferreira, May 2026). The names matter less
than the model they encode:

1. **AEO is output-focused** — *how do I get selected as the answer?* It targets the final
   citation step: being named in the visible output.
2. **LLM SEO is channel-focused** — *how do I do SEO, but for LLMs?* Same playbook as SEO,
   applied to a new surface. Same unit (pages), same mental model.
3. **GEO is pipeline-focused** — *how does my content survive retrieval, extraction,
   compression, and citation before an answer is even generated?* Content that isn't
   retrieved never reaches citation; content retrieved but unclearly extractable gets
   paraphrased into something unrecognizable; content that extracts cleanly but compresses
   poorly loses its core claim.

The unit shift this implies is the structural claim worth adopting: **pages for SEO/AEO,
sections for GEO.** The GEO Lab's version: "a page that ranks first but has no clearly
extractable sections gives the retrieval system nothing to work with. It ranks. It does not
participate in the answer." Ahrefs' December 2025 analysis put a number on the same gap: AI
Overviews reduce organic CTR by roughly 58% for top-ranking pages that aren't cited in the
summary. First page, zero participation — that is the SEO-into-GEO gap in one statistic
(as reported in the GEO Lab's breakdown).

## Where the taxonomy breaks down in 2026

Three tensions define the current landscape, and being honest about them is half the value of
this article.

**1. Google says it's all SEO; the academy and vendors disagree.** Google's own AI
optimization guidance calls AEO and GEO "market terms" and instructs firms that for Google
Search, "the work is still SEO" — including dismissive takes on chunking, llms.txt, and
inauthentic mentions. Academic work went the other direction: the 2024 KDD GEO paper
(Aggarwal et al.) found adding citations, direct quotes and statistics lifted visibility in
generated answers up to ~40% (with the headline citation lift ~+115% in their controlled
benchmark); the C-SEO Bench follow-up (Puerto et al.) found most "conversational SEO"
tactics don't help and several hurt. Google's position and the vendors' position both contain
a truth, because generative visibility lives in the retrieval layer and the SEO foundation
that feeds it.

**2. LLMO is a rebrand until it isn't.** The 2026 literature commonly treats GEO and LLMO as
the same work ("a rolling rebrand of the same field," per Neil Patel; "AEO and GEO are the
same thing," per Profound), while the tools sell "LLMO" as a differentiator. The legitimate
distinct meaning is genuinely model-layer: cross-model prompt panels (does brand recall
persist across GPT, Claude and Gemini?), training-data presence audits (is the model aware of
you *without* a web fetch?), and token-level citation analysis (what content shape gets quoted
verbatim?). If the vendor can't name the model layer, the data boundary, and the controlled
intervention, the distinction is marketing (appwebseo, 2026).

**3. The entire taxonomy is Western-engines-shaped.** Eastbound's May 2026 audit found the
practitioner vocabulary defaults to ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and AI Overviews —
but Chinese engines (DeepSeek, Qwen, Doubao) rely on a source substrate that differs by
70–80%, with a 540-call panel showing top-15 source overlap (Jaccard) of 0.20–0.30 between
*any two Chinese engines*, far lower against Western ones. Zhihu not Reddit, Xiaohongshu not
YouTube, Baidu Baike not Wikipedia. None of the four terms carry that caveat by default; we
flag it rather than silently scope-creep around it.

## What the 2026 measurement says (the part marketing skips)

Terms resolve fastest when you look at the measurement, and two 2026 studies cut through the
definitions:

- **Pre-training bias, not just retrieval.** Chen, Wang, Chen & Koudas (arXiv 2601.16858)
  compared GPT-4o, Claude and Perplexity against Google + Gemini across popular and niche
  entities. Two findings: AI systems bias heavily toward earned media while Google keeps a
  balanced owned-and-earned mix; and for *popular* entities, LLM pre-training dominates —
  rankings were nearly invariant to snippet shuffling and entity swapping (citation-miss
  rates ~0.06 for Toyota, ~0.73 for Infiniti), because retrieval merely *confirms* what the
  model already "knows." For niche entities, context matters enormously. Implication: the
  SEO foundation and earned-media coverage determine retrieval, but for well-known brands the
  model's own priors decide naming — the "entity recognition gates everything" result from
  our [entity article](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026) reproduced at the
  taxonomy level.
- **Multiples are inflated by platform tailwind.** Watanabe & Nakayashiki (arXiv 2606.04362)
  ran a natural experiment on a high-traffic domain: AEO-treated pages saw ChatGPT referral
  growth of 6.1× raw — but untreated pages on the *same domain* grew 3.5× under the identical
  platform tailwind, and the defensible, platform-adjusted treatment effect was roughly
  1.8–2.3× and still merely suggestive under the most conservative test. Their conclusion is
  the one to internalize: "headline AEO multiples substantially overstate causal effect,"
  and on-domain controls are the standard correction. Whoever quotes you a giant GEO/AEO
  multiple without a control group is quoting you a tailwind.

Those two findings do the real work of this article: they tell you where each discipline is
actually the bottleneck — retrieval-support and earned media for GEO (lever 1–4 and 9–10 in
the [11-levers hub](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026)), model-layer representation
for LLMO, and extractability for AEO.

## The decision rule

1. **Start with SEO.** It is the foundation and the only discipline with a settled,
   measurable surface. High rankings also double as AI-eligibility insurance (appwebseo:
   eligibility → retrieval → mention → citation → visit → revenue — no step is guaranteed).
2. **Layer AEO + GEO as one initiative.** Restructure extractable answer blocks (AEO),
   tighten structure and schema, then add the evidence and entity layer (GEO). One set of
   page moves serves both — because they share the pipeline we described.
3. **Treat LLMO as a specific, model-layer mandate.** Prompt panels across engines; measure
   whether your brand and claims survive *without* retrieval. That test tells you whether
   your knowledge-graph presence (see
   [entity optimization](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026)) is working.
4. **Watch AAO, don't invest yet.** The agent economy is early; the prerequisites are the
   data and entity foundations you already need.
5. **Never choose by the buzzword vendor.** Name the surface, name the intervention, name the
   metric, name the accountable owner. If you can't, the label is doing the steering — and
   the label is the least reliable part of the stack.
