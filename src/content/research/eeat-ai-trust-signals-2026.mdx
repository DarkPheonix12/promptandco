  2026 AI engines practice it. Google's E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness,
  Trustworthiness) is a rubric for *human quality raters*; it was never a numbered ranking
  factor. AI engines cannot interview your author or verify a certification — so they

When marketers say "E-E-A-T matters now more than ever," they are usually describing a
situation that has actually changed shape. The framework did not get stronger — it got
*executable*. Google's own documentation is explicit: E-E-A-T describes "a mix of factors"
raters use to identify helpful content, is not itself a ranking factor, and gives more
weight to "Your Money or Your Life" topics. Separately, Google's 2026 AI-search guidance
calls AEO and GEO "market terms" and answers that for Google Search "the work is still SEO."
Neither statement kills E-E-A-T. Both statements push the *why* questions — who, how, why
was this made? — toward the mechanism that actually decides retrieval: does the page make
its verifiability machine-readable?

This article is the evidence map for that mechanism, and its honesty boundary.

## The four pillars, operationalized

| Pillar | What a rater does | What a machine parses | 2026 evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Experience** | Looks for first-hand involvement | Original data only this brand holds; first-person method; declared audience | Proprietary/original data ranks as the single highest-impact trust signal in the 77-signal taxonomy (9.5/10; self-reported, see caveats); original-research pages dominate the most-cited set in engine analyses |
| **Expertise** | Verifies credentials | A resolvable Person entity: stable `@id`, browsable about page, credentials, `sameAs` links to profiles the model already knows | Named authorship with credentials scores 7.5/10 (SIGI taxonomy); author-entity resolution is the "who is claiming this?" check every engine runs after retrieval |
| **Authoritativeness** | Weighs recognition | Third-party corroboration: brand mentions, listings, reviews, coverage across sources the engine already retrieves | Branded mentions correlate roughly 0.50–0.74 with AI visibility versus below 0.30 for backlinks (Ahrefs); unlinked mentions ~0.67 in a separate analysis; ~85% of non-paid citations come from earned media (2026 syntheses) |
| **Trustworthiness** | Reads for accuracy and transparency | Verifiability stack: methodology, citation arrays, visible dates, editorial-standards disclosure | Methodology sections score highest among authority-provenance signals (8.5/10); cited sources average 8–12 external citations vs 2–3 for uncited (100-piece paired study) |

The far-right column is where the discipline lives now. Everything below explains what holds
up under scrutiny and what is still contested.

## Provenance is the cheapest trust lever

The most consistent 2026 finding is that *verifiability* — not prominence, not polish — is
the signal that travels across engines. Two independent lines of evidence converge:

- **Structure-level.** In the GEO-16 audit of 1,702 citations, the on-page pillars most
  strongly associated with citation were Metadata & Freshness (r = 0.68), Semantic HTML
  (0.65), and Structured Data (0.63), with Evidence & Citations (0.61) and Authority & Trust
  (0.59) close behind; pages scoring ≥ 0.70 across ≥ 12 pillars hit a 78% cross-engine
  citation rate. These are page-level, shippable-in-weeks changes.
- **Content-level.** A 100-cited-versus-100-uncited comparison across ChatGPT, Perplexity
  and Google AI Overviews found cited sources averaged 8–12 external citations and regular
  update dates, versus 2–3 for uncited ones, and that pages missing more than two of the
  five minimum trust signals failed mainly on trust itself. The single most-cited trust
  asset was original research: primary sources were cited at dramatically higher rates than
  aggregators, an effect flozi's analysis formalizes as a tier system with proprietary data
  at the top.

That convergence is not a coincidence. The SIGI trust-taxonomy work proposes the mechanism:
provenance signals act as *uncertainty reducers* — the model cites what it can verify because
every wrong citation is reputationally expensive to the engine. Named authors reduce the
"who even wrote this?" cost; methodology answers "how does this source know?"; inline
citations let the engine check the claim chain before it commits to quoting you. In the
same study, the model self-reported that the top 10 of 77 signals account for ~80% of
estimated citation impact, and 9 of the 10 highest-impact signals operate at the inference
layer — meaning most of the leverage is page-level and controllable, not a long brand build.

Those exact findings are hypothesis-grade (single model, self-reported) — a limitation we
flag because the entire discipline can oversell one model's introspection.

## Corroboration outranks links

The authority lever shifted from the link graph to the mention graph:

- Ahrefs' 2025–26 brand correlation work: branded web *mentions* and video impressions
  correlate with AI visibility at roughly 0.50–0.74, while backlinks and ad spend sit below
  0.30. The December 2025 follow-up pushed the YouTube-impression line to ~0.74.
- A Search Engine Journal analysis (November 2025) put unlinked brand mentions at ~0.67
  correlation with AI Overview citations — above raw backlink counts.
- A University-of-Toronto research finding, widely reproduced in 2026 syntheses: earned
  media outperforms brand-owned content for citation rates by roughly 3×.
- Yext's 6.8-million-citation analysis found ~86% of AI citations come from brand-managed
  sources (44% first-party sites, 42% business listings, 8% reviews/social), with community
  forums near 2% once location and intent were factored in.

Read separately these look like competing claims about who gets cited. Read together they
say the same thing our [entity article](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026)
established: the deciding factor is *recognition plus corroboration*, and engines lean on
sources other people have already vouched for. The listings 42% line is exactly why the
Google Business Profile matters for AI visibility even though Google's docs say there are "no
special requirements" — local engines separately consume it.

## What is still honestly contested

No credible article on trust in AI search skips these four flags:

1. **E-E-A-T was never a numbered factor.** Google's raters have "no control over how pages
   rank," and the mechanism between rater feedback and ranking systems is not publicly
   documented. Any tool selling "your E-E-A-T score is X" is selling a proxy at best.
2. **Vendor effect sizes are unverifiable.** Figures like a "40.9% named-expert lift" or
   "a 0.76 Knowledge Graph correlation with AI citation" circulate widely without traceable
   primary sources (SEOHack's 2026 audit graded exactly this class of claim "Tier 3 — no
   independent verification found"). The canonical, peer-reviewed numbers remain the KDD
   2024 GEO experiment: citations +115%, direct quotes +43%, named statistics +33%. We use
   those; treat the rest as directional.
3. **Trust in the source does not fix fidelity.** The largest AIO audit of 2026 (55,393
   queries; 98,020 atomic claims) found 11.0% of claims unsupported by their cited pages —
   with *omission* the dominant failure mode — and found source quality and claim fidelity
   largely independent. The same study found AIO-cited domains are *more* credible than
   co-displayed first-page results (PC1 ≈ 0.73 vs 0.65), yet ~30% of cited domains do not
   appear in first-page results at all. Citations function as a visibility-allocation
   mechanism, per the peer-reviewed Springer CRD analysis, not purely as transparency cues.
4. **Engine trust layers differ.** Google's AI Search runs on its ranking index and
   Knowledge Graph; ChatGPT leans on training-time knowledge plus retrieval; Perplexity is
   retrieval-first; Claude performs live page fetches. In GEO-16, engines even cited
   different *quality thresholds* (Brave 0.73, AI Overviews 0.69, Perplexity 0.30 mean). One
   trust foundation must serve several different selection logics — the reason entity-level
   work pays across all of them.

## The trust build, sequenced

1. **Week 1–2 (eligibility).** Verify AI crawler access; add visibility dates and recency
   metadata. These are the binary gates — absent means never retrieved.
2. **Week 2–6 (structure).** Add a resolvable author Person entity with a real about page
   and `sameAs`; state the critical facts of every contextually important page declaratively
   (who it is for, what it asserts, what backs it up). Add a methodology section where any
   claim of research exists. This is [claude-style live-fetch](/resources/research/seo-geo-aeo-llmo-guide-2026)
   trust: what reads as rigorous reads as citable.
3. **Month 2–6 (corroboration).** The slow lever. Align the facts on your site with every
   listing, profile and review surface (entity consistency), then earn mentions on sources
   the engines already retrieve — the [entity-chain build](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026)
   in parallel with [earned coverage](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026).
4. **Ongoing (audit).** Re-benchmark quarterly with brand-name prompts and citation tracking
   using the [measurement guide's](/resources/research/ai-visibility-measurement-buyers-guide)
   battery. Track whether engines *name and quote* you, not just whether they describe your
   category.

The honest timeline is the SEOHack one: implementation of the machine-readable trust stack
takes 4–6 weeks with measurable output; earned authority takes 6–18 months with compounding.
New sites can clear the retrieval layer and earn a first citation within about three months
on a deliberate program. Nothing in 2026 makes trust instant; the shift is that it is now
*a build you can do*, rather than a reputation you hope someone attributes to you.
