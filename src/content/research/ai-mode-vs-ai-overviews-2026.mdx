In the [11-levers model](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026), Google is the largest
single distribution channel for AI answers — and it is the one most people treat as a single
channel. By mid-2026 that is wrong. Google ships at least two answer surfaces with materially
different retrieval pipelines, citation behavior, and source preferences: **AI Overviews**, the
lighter box under a subset of queries, and **AI Mode**, the two-column, multi-turn experience
that became the default for some query classes after Google I/O in May 2026. This article is
the honest treatment of the gap between them, the evidence that quantifies it, and the dual-
surface play that survives it. The per-engine story beyond Google (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini,
Copilot) gets its own treatment in our flagship study later; here the question is narrower and
stranger: *why does the same company's two answer engines disagree about the same query?*

## The paradox: near-identical answers, different receipts

Start with what the two surfaces agree on. Ahrefs' December 2025 study (Despina Gavoyannis and
Xibeijia Guan, using 730,000 query pairs from US Brand Radar, September 2025) found AI Mode and
AI Overviews produce semantically similar answers **86% of the time** — 89.7% of the pairs scored
above 0.8 on cosine similarity. For users, the answer is effectively the same.

Then look at the receipts: of those same query pairs, only **13.7% of citation URLs overlap**
(16.3% for the top-three cited sources per query). Word-level overlap (Jaccard) is about 16%.
Same first sentence, 2.51%. Identical responses, 0.51%.

One stat is why practitioners trust Google to be "one engine"; the other is why the engines keep
citing different vendors for the same question. Both are true. Almost everything useful about AI
Mode in 2026 is inside the space between them.

## The measurement reconciliation: two engines wearing one coat

The public debate over "the real overlap number" is a units argument wearing a data-set
argument's clothes. Replicate studies cluster tightly *once you line up the measurement*:

| Study | Unit | Overlap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ahrefs (Dec 2025), 540k query pairs | citation URL, per query | 13.7% |
| Ahrefs, top-3 sources | citation URL, per query | 16.3% |
| Finseo (Sep 2026), 42,937 prompt-days | URL / domain, per query | 13.7% / 18.7% |
| SE Ranking | URL / domain, per query | ~10.7% / ~16% |
| Victorious, 1,540 queries | any exact citation match | 0 matches; 77% of domains on one surface only |
| **BrightEdge AI Catalyst (Apr 2026)** | **top-100 cited domains, all queries** | **AIO vs AI Mode ≈ 59% — the *highest* pair** |

Read that last row carefully, because it resolves the confusion. Per query, AI Overviews and AI
Mode cite *different* things. But if you aggregate each surface's most-cited 100 domains across
many queries, the two surfaces are the **most similar pair** in the field — more than Gemini is
to either (34% vs AI Overviews, 27% vs AI Mode) and more than Gemini is to ChatGPT (39%).

The reconciliation: **the divergence is at the query level, not the ecosystem level.** Same
wardrobe, different engines. A vendor trying to force "one Google footprint" via aggregate
visibility numbers will see convergence and conclude the surfaces are interchangeable. The same
vendor, watching a single buyer-query, sees 86% different sources and correctly cannot optimize
both surfaces with one set of signals. Both views are right; they are measuring different units.
Quoting a single "overlap percentage" for "Google AI" without specifying the unit is how the
confusion reproduces. (For the same units trap in citation tracking generally, the
[measurement buyer's guide](/resources/research/ai-visibility-measurement-buyers-guide) has the
full treatment.)

## Why they diverge: query fan-out and passage-level scoring

The divergence is not a bug; the two pipelines are built differently.

- **Query fan-out.** AI Mode splits a query into multiple parallel sub-questions and answers
  each passage-level. Google's own guidance confirms AI Overviews and AI Mode "may use different
  models and techniques" to answer a query. More sub-questions, more worlds to pull sources from.
- **More sources, fewer gaps.** AI Mode cites in ~97% of responses, AI Overviews in ~89% (Ahrefs),
  so the no-citation gap is rarer there. On URLs per answer, Finseo counted AI Mode at ~10.8 versus
  AI Overviews at ~6.3; ApiSerpent/Adamarant data puts the pair at ~9.2 versus ~7.7.
- **Passage scoring, not page scoring.** AI Mode scores passages against the unseen
  sub-questions, so a mid-page passage or a niche database can win a citation that the overall
  page would never earn in AI Overviews. This is the closest thing to old passage-based
  (Maximized Answer) indexing in the current stack — and it rewards different page shapes.
- **Entity multiplication.** Ahrefs found AI Mode's answers are roughly **4x longer** with
  **3.3 entities versus 1.3** in a paired answer, and that it includes the AI Overviews entities
  plus *more* about 61% of the time. Brand carry from the light surface to the heavy one is
  real — but it arrives with more competitors.

## The source-type divide: who leans where

The two surfaces have distinct dietary preferences. Ahrefs' source-share deltas, plus MR
Research's July 2026 ratio analysis (AI Mode share ÷ AI Overviews share), give a consistent
picture:

| Source type | Lean | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Video (YouTube) | AI Overviews | cited ~2x vs AI Mode; YouTube is AIO's top domain |
| Community (Reddit) | roughly even | similar frequency on both; see our [community piece](/resources/research/reddit-community-ai-citations-2026) |
| Wikipedia | AI Mode | 28.9% of AI Mode citations vs 18.1% in AIO |
| Quora | AI Mode | ~3.5x AIO's share |
| Health / medical | AI Mode | ~2x AIO's share |
| Facebook | AI Mode | ~2x AIO's share |
| Market research / databases | AI Mode | G2 1.81:1, Grand View Research 1.90:1, MarketsandMarkets 1.63:1, Mordor 1.60:1 |
| Wire services / consulting | AI Overviews | PR Newswire 0.67:1, Deloitte 0.71:1 (ratio below 1 = AIO-leaning) |

Weird but repeatable: the *reference layer* (Wikipedia, Quora, databases) feeds AI Mode, while
the *broadcast layer* (video, wired press) feeds AI Overviews. If you are a software vendor, the
practical read is that your G2 or comparison profile is disproportionately a Gemini/AI Mode
asset, while your YouTube and press coverage are disproportionately an AI Overviews asset. The
[entity layer](/resources/research/entity-optimization-2026) and the
[earned-media layer](/resources/research/earned-media-ai-search-playbook-2026) belong on
different surfaces' menus.

## Volatility and the snapshot caveat

Every overlap figure in this article is a snapshot. Ahrefs flagged that roughly **45% of an AI
Overview's citations change between generations**; BrightEdge's 16-month run shows AI
Overviews/organic citation overlap drifting from **32.3% to 54.5%** while 45.5% of citations
still come from pages outside the organic top-10. The May 2026 I/O "merge" throughline is mostly
a *UI* merge — AI Mode surfacing as the default for more queries — and the behavioral split
between the two retrieval pipelines persists where it matters. Add the wider fact that
cross-engine zeroshared-domain queries run 35–40% (Search Atlas, 5.5M responses) and the honest
conclusion is: treat any single-number overlap figure as dated, label its unit, and re-baseline
your own tracking quarterly. QuickSEO's observation that two engines disagree about a brand's
presence 62% of the time stops being a measurement error once you accept the divergence is
structural.

## The dual-surface playbook

Given two engines, one of which aggregates to the same top-100 as the other but never in the
same query, the defensible play has four moves:

1. **Split your optimization target, not your Google budget.** Structure and passage clarity for
   AI Mode; video, community threads, and press mentions for AI Overviews; entity + Wikipedia
   depth for both. They are different muscle groups.
2. **Track AI Mode and AI Overviews separately — and log the 'diverged' state.** Being cited on
   one surface and absent on the other is a signal, not a bug. It tells you which of your assets
   the other surface is not seeing, which is a specific, amenable problem.
3. **Play the carry, price the competition.** The 61% brand-carry means AI Mode often adopts your
   AI Overviews citation — but only as part of a longer, more crowded answer. Your AI Overviews
   win is your AI Mode baseline, never your AI Mode finish line.
4. **Measure revenue, not clicks.** Seer Interactive (3,119 queries, 42 clients, 25.1M
   impressions, Jun 2024–Sep 2025) measured organic CTR falling from **1.76% to 0.61%** when an
   AI Overview is present — a 61% decline — a finding that has only aged in one direction. If your
   dashboard still rewards clicks, both surfaces will look like failure even when they are
   converting.

## Bottom line

Google's two answer surfaces say the same thing and cite different people. The 13.7% per-query
URL overlap coexists with a 59% aggregate top-100 similarity because **their divergence lives at
query level, not ecosystem level** — same coat, two engines, different retrieval pipelines. That
reconciliation is the practical takeaway: stop quoting a single "Google overlap number," track
each surface separately, and allocate by source-type preference — passage and reference depth for
AI Mode, video and community and press for AI Overviews. Pair it with the
[11-levers hub](/resources/research/ai-citation-levers-2026) and the
[community thread](/resources/research/reddit-community-ai-citations-2026), and watch this space
for the five-engine divergence study that maps the same question across everything else.
